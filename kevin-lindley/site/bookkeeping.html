Bookkeeping Cleanup Services for Books That Have Fallen Behind

Catch-up bookkeeping is where a lot of new clients start. The first year of any business is messy, because you're paying for things before you're making money, usually on personal cards.

We review what's there and reconcile every account back to the bank statements. Anything coded wrong gets reclassified. Expenses you paid personally go on a simple spreadsheet you send us, and we record them properly.

Then we set the accounts up correctly, so the same cleanup isn't needed again next year.

What We Check in Your Books Every Month

Say you open QuickBooks in June and the business shows a profit you know can't be right. Usually the money is all there, but some of it has been recorded in the wrong place, like a loan payment booked as an expense or a transfer to savings counted as spending.

These are the things we look at every time we reconcile your accounts.

Every account ties to the bank

The balance in QuickBooks has to match the bank statement. The bank statement is the one record we know is right, so every reconciliation starts there.

Owner money, loans and distributions

Money you put into the business, money you take out and loan payments all look like ordinary transactions to the software. We classify each one correctly, because the return treats them very differently.

Expenses paid from the wrong account

A business expense on a personal card happens to everyone, especially in the first year. Send us a quick note with the date, the amount and what it was for, and we record it with a journal entry so it still counts as a business expense.

Equipment, debt and contractors

Larger purchases like computers and equipment are set up so they can be depreciated properly. Loan balances are checked against the lender's statement, and payments to contractors are tracked so they're ready for year-end reporting.

Revenue that matches what you collected

Billing software doesn't always agree with what actually hit the bank. We tie the two together and split revenue by source, so you can see which part of the business is carrying the load.

Payroll split the way you read it

If you have staff, we can separate wages by role, like front office and the people who bill for their time, so you can see what each part of the team costs.

Once everything is in the right place, the books start doing real work for you.

How Your Books Feed the Tax Projection

Early in the year we build your tax road map with round numbers, because nobody knows exactly how the year will go. As the months pass, the books replace those guesses with what actually happened.

By the December projection, January through November is already in the books and we're only estimating the last month. That's what makes the final estimated payment accurate, and it's why a December conversation is so much more useful than an April one.

The books also answer the retirement question. If you've been setting money aside for a contribution and you're not sure how much to put in, we look at what the business actually earned and tell you what to fund.

Bookkeeping Questions We Get Asked

Do I need to keep receipts for every business expense?
−

No. If everything runs through a dedicated business account, the bank statement shows most of what an auditor needs. Keep receipts for the big purchases, like office equipment or a laptop, where you'd need to show it was for the business. The IRS recordkeeping guidance puts the burden of proof on you, and the large items are what matter most.

Can you clean up books that are a year or more behind?
−

Yes. Send whatever you have, even if it's a pile of statements nobody has opened. We reconcile each account back to the bank, fix the coding, and get you current. It's more work up front, and after that it's routine.

Do I need a separate business bank account?
−

Yes. If you're ever audited and the IRS asks where the numbers came from, you point to one account. If business and personal money are mixed, they'll want to look through your personal account too. It's like airport security. You have nothing to hide, but you don't want someone digging through your whole bag.

How often do the books get updated?
−

Monthly or quarterly, depending on the business and how often you want to see the numbers. We'll recommend what fits when we put the quote together.

What does catch-up bookkeeping cost?
−

It's quoted separately from the monthly work. The price depends on how many months are behind, how many accounts there are and how many transactions need to be classified. Once you're current, the monthly fee takes over.

Do I need QuickBooks at all?
−

Not always. If you're a sole proprietor and everything runs through a dedicated business account, a simple month-by-month spreadsheet can be enough. Once you have an S-corp, payroll or staff, real bookkeeping software is worth it, and QuickBooks fits most businesses our size. Much larger practices sometimes need something more sophisticated, and we'll tell you if you're there.
