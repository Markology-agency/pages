Buying a dental practice? Send us the P&L before you sign

Most buyers fall in love with the schedule. It's booked out for weeks and the production report looks great. Then we open the P&L and ask where the money went.

Book a call with the Virjee Consulting team

The question we ask on every deal

If you bought this practice, put an associate in the chair at a normal production percentage, and went to Hawaii for four years, would there still be money in the bank when you got back?

If yes, you bought a business. If the numbers only work when you're the one producing every day, Omar calls that golden handcuffs. You paid for a job you can't leave.

What we look at before the LOI

Owner pay. A seller paying themselves well under market makes EBITDA look like a smash hit. We back out what the owner takes and put in what you'd pay a doctor to do that production. The profit left over is what the practice is really earning.

The lease. Wages, marketing, lab and supplies can all be adjusted. Rent can't. A heavy lease on a smaller practice is the one we most often tell first-time buyers to walk away from, because it also makes the practice harder to sell later.

Collections versus production. A full schedule means little if the collections aren't there.

New patients versus growth. If the practice adds plenty of new patients and spends real money on marketing but revenue is flat, patients are leaving out the back door.

Costs that live somewhere else. When an owner with several offices sells one, lab, supplies or the office manager are sometimes paid by the office that isn't for sale. The P&L looks lean for the wrong reason.

The real cost to get it where you want it. Digital charts, a CBCT, signage, leasehold improvements. The purchase price is only the start of what you're investing.

The bank said yes. Isn't that enough?

The bank is checking whether you can make the loan payment. That's a good first box to tick. It doesn't tell you whether the practice will grow in value, or whether you could sell it if life changed and you needed out.

After you buy

How the price is split between goodwill, equipment and other assets, and when you elect S-corp status, both change what you owe for years. We set that up with you before closing, so it isn't a surprise at your first tax return.

Questions we hear a lot

When should I send you the numbers?

Before you sign the letter of intent. Once the LOI is signed, the price is mostly set and what's left to negotiate gets smaller.

What do you need from me?

The seller's last three years of P&Ls and tax returns, a recent production and collections report, and the lease. If you only have the broker's summary, send that and we'll tell you what to ask for.

Do you work with my broker and attorney?

Yes. They handle the deal and the legal side. We handle the financial side and the tax structure.
