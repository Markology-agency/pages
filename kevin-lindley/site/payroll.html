Payroll That Fits the Tax Plan

The right salary

An S-corp owner has to be paid reasonable compensation before taking distributions. We set a salary that holds up, balanced against the tax savings and how much you want to put into retirement. Your retirement contributions are tied to that salary, so it's a lever we can raise or lower through the year.

The right setup

Federal and state registrations, the unemployment accounts, and the payroll provider, configured so the quarterly Form 941 filings and year-end W-2s happen on their own.

If your kids do real work for the business, paying them can be a legitimate strategy, and their earnings can go toward a Roth IRA in their name.

Payroll Questions We Get Asked

What counts as a reasonable salary for an S-corp owner?+

The IRS requires S-corp owners to take reasonable compensation for the work they do before taking distributions. There's no single formula. We look at your role, your profession, what the business earns, and how much you want to put into retirement, then set a salary that fits the facts and that I'm comfortable defending.

Can I pay my kids through the business?+

Yes, if they do real work and the pay is reasonable for that work. The rules for family employees differ depending on whether your business is a sole proprietorship, a partnership, or a corporation. We set it up to match your entity and keep the records to support it.

Does it matter if there's no payroll set up yet when electing S-corp status?+

It's not ideal, but it's fixable. A W-2 is an annual form, so payroll can technically catch up later in the year. I'd still rather have payroll running well before year end, so there's working capital in the business and the salary isn't one big December catch-up. We usually file Form 2553 and set up payroll together.

How do I get paid out of my S-corp?+

Two ways. A salary through payroll is subject to income tax and payroll taxes. Distributions are subject to income tax only. The salary has to be reasonable first, then the rest can come out as distributions. We plan that split every year.
