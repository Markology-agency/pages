The Person You'll Work With

I'm a licensed CPA and I've been doing this for more than a decade. I'm not in love with taxes, but I do like helping people, and I like talking to them. Most of my clients are physicians, practice owners, and other professionals whose income comes from more than one place.

I have a few people who help me part-time with bookkeeping and basic prep. I'm still the one you talk to, from the first call to the filed return.

I've also got three kids, so I understand how little free time an owner has. It's part of why the firm is intentionally small. I'm not trying to scale it forever. I'd rather know every client's situation well.

what makes us different

What Makes Working With Us Different

At a mid-size firm, you get one of two problems. Either there are too many cooks in the kitchen, or nobody takes ownership. Here, the answer to "who owns this?" is one person.

The work itself comes in three parts. First is compliance, which means getting the numbers right and filing on time. Second is tax strategy: figuring out what the IRS actually permits you to deduct, and doing it. Third is education. I want you to understand what you have and why, so none of it feels like it works by magic.

We reach out first

A tax road map early in the year, a projection in December, and the questions in between.

We're glad when you call

A reasonable amount of consultation is in the fee. You won't get a bill for every hour like an attorney would send you.

Every deduction has a reason behind it

If I can't justify it in an audit, it's off the table. I'll tell you plainly which is which.

why i still like this work

Why I Still Like This Work

If you spend any time on social media, you’ve probably seen someone telling business owners to put everything in a trust and stop paying tax. I have a strange passion for accounting, and a big part of it is how much bad CPA work I see out there.

Most of the problems I find come from small things nobody explained. It might be a salary set too low, a loan coded as income, or a home office nobody claimed because the last accountant called it a red flag. The fix is usually a plan with a clear reason behind each piece of it. That’s also why the firm is built the way it is.

small on purpose

Small on Purpose

A lot of CPA firms have a partner who runs the engagement and a manager who’s supposed to do the work, and clients end up passed around between them. I kept Kestrel small so that doesn’t happen to you.

The person who builds your plan in February is the person who answers your text in July.

There’s usually a busy stretch at the start, when you might talk to me five times in a month while we get the books, payroll and road map set up. After that things settle down, and we talk when something changes. Both are normal, and both are part of the fee.

Keeping it small also means I only take on as many clients as I can actually know well. When you call, I already know your situation and we can get straight to the question.
