Schwartz & Associates CPA: SEO audit

www.rschwartzcpa.com · October 23, 2026 · three weeks after the new site went live on September 30

Summary

SEO health score: 69 out of 100 (needs improvement). Business type: local service business (CPA firm, one office in Turnersville, NJ).

The new site is technically sound. All 93 pages in the sitemap load, Google has indexed the key pages, and nothing is blocked. Organic clicks are flat because of four things that the rebuild didn't fix or that it made worse:

  1. The homepage carries the whole site. It ranks for nearly every money search, while the service pages sit at positions 24 to 60. Only the Google Business listing (the map) brings meaningful clicks.
  2. About 10 old pages that ranked near page one were merged into one thin page. The old town pages (Mount Laurel, Medford, Maple Shade, Voorhees, Washington Township, Moorestown and Cherry Hill) ranked at positions 9 to 17. The Philadelphia page had 19,117 impressions in 90 days and ranked 6.8 for "small business tax preparers". They now all redirect to /south-jersey-cpa/, which has 419 words and one internal link pointing to it.
  3. The blog doesn't earn clicks. 62 posts had 7,963 impressions and 25 clicks in 90 days. No post sits on page one for a search that matters.
  4. The searches Schwartz can win outside "near me" are small. "small business accountant nj" gets about 140 searches a month, and Schwartz already ranks first for it nationally. The volume is in "cpa near me" (2,900 a month) and "accountant near me" (1,900), and the map pack decides those.

Content quality: 60

  • /south-jersey-cpa/ has 419 words. It absorbed the old town pages and the Philadelphia page, which together had about 20,400 impressions in 90 days.
  • /pricing/ has 109 words of body copy around the price matcher. Google can't see the matcher's results, so the page doesn't answer "how much does a CPA cost".

E-E-A-T sprint: what we shipped on October 23

  • Organization schema on the homepage now lists the legal name, address, phone, service area (Gloucester, Camden and Burlington counties, South Jersey, New Jersey), 18 areas of expertise, the founder and team leads, PASBA and NJCPA memberships, and the Facebook, Instagram and LinkedIn profiles.
  • Person schema for Rich Schwartz, CPA and Ling Ji, CPA on the About page, with job titles, CPA credentials, memberships and LinkedIn profiles.
  • A duplicate organization block that had been typed into the About page body was removed. Its details now live in the schema above.
  • Common Questions sections (14 Q&As across 5 posts) with matching FAQ schema. The posts are comfort letters for mortgages, year-end vs monthly accounting, bookkeeping vs accounting, mixing personal and business money, and switching tax preparers. Every answer comes from the post itself.
  • A stale paragraph was corrected on the comfort letter post, the blog's top post by impressions. It said the American Families Plan and American Jobs Plan "will bring with them new taxes on businesses in the coming year". Neither became law. It now ends with a short note on what to do when a lender asks for a CPA letter.
  • llms.txt is live at /llms.txt. It lists every page and post, plus a plain description of the firm, team, memberships and services.
  • Bing verification tag and IndexNow are switched on through Rank Math. Bing Webmaster Tools still needs the site added, and the IndexNow key file still needs uploading to the site root.

What we still need from the firm: a short line on each team member's experience (for team schema and the industry pages), and one real client story per industry page. The industry pages are the ones ChatGPT already cites, and first-hand detail is what keeps them cited.

Decision needed: town pages

The rebuild consolidated the town pages because near-identical town pages could put the #1 map position at risk. The data now shows the old town pages ranked at positions 9 to 17 and brought clicks (Maple Shade 3, Mount Laurel 2, Voorhees 2). A middle path is 4 to 5 town pages, each with genuinely different content (local clients served, local roads and landmarks, distance from the office, a local review). They would cover only towns where the old page had clicks. This is Whitney's call.
