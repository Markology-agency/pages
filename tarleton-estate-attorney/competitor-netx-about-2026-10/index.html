What we recommend testing

Fix first

Pick one number for years in practice

The bio's meta description says "30+ years." The homepage says "more than 16 years." We'll confirm the right figure with Allan and use it everywhere.

Test 1

Add Allan's own "why" to the bio

Add a short first-person section built from Allan's own words. It might cover the Marines, his family, or why he moved into estate work. We'll take it from a call or a quick questionnaire and won't write it for him. Measure: clicks from the bio to the consultation form over 60 days, compared with the 60 days before. Constraint: Texas Bar advertising rules, and no promised outcomes.

Test 2

Link his credentials to the source

Link to his board certification listing and his State Bar of Texas profile, and add both to his page schema. Anyone who checks, including AI search tools, can then confirm the certification. NETX can't match it. Measure: whether AI answers about Dallas estate attorneys mention Allan. We'll check monthly.

Test 3

Put Terrell on the bio

Change the title to name both Dallas and Terrell. Add one line on the Kaufman County office, matching how the rest of the site presents both locations. Measure: Terrell location searches in Search Console.

Search update since September

NETX's power of attorney page still brings in most of their search traffic. It has slipped from #12 to #15 for "power of attorney texas" (4,400 searches a month). Tarleton's new power of attorney page is now live. We'll track both pages on that phrase every week.

What we didn't check

This review covers their website and SEMrush only. We haven't checked NETX's Google reviews, their ads in Google's or Meta's ad libraries, or their social accounts. NETX's conversion numbers are private, so we can't say whether the story on their About page brings them clients. The tests above are how we find out for Allan.
