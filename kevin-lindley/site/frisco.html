Starting Out, and Trying to Do It Right the First Time

The people I talk to at this stage are usually confident about the work itself. The business side is new.

The contract wants an entity formed. The attorney is drafting documents, and you'd like a financial opinion before they finish. Meanwhile you're paying startup costs on a personal card because the business account isn't open yet.

That's normal. You might not see yourself as a startup, but that's what's going on. You're building infrastructure, and it takes a while to get it running.

What I want to avoid is the cleanup later. I have folks on my to-do list whose setup has been off for two years. Getting the order right up front is much easier than fixing it after.

Setting Up a New Business in Frisco, in Order

1. The entity

An LLC or PLLC, formed with the Texas Secretary of State by you, an attorney, or an online service. I don't form it myself. I'm happy to get on a call with your attorney so the legal and tax sides line up.

2. The business bank account

A dedicated account and card from day one. If the IRS ever asks where the income came from, you point to that account. Your personal account stays out of it.

3. The S-corp question

We answer it with the math, not by default. Filing Form 2553 only takes a few minutes. Deciding whether it's worth it is the real work, and we often wait until revenue shows where the year is heading.

4. Payroll and registrations

Owner payroll when the S-corp makes sense, and staff payroll once there's staff. I set it up in the payroll software, and you or your office manager can run it from there.

5. The books and the road map

A QuickBooks file tied to the business account, and a tax road map from the first month. That way the first year's tax bill is planned for.

Where the First Year Usually Goes Sideways

The first year is almost always messy. You're spending before the revenue shows up, and some of it goes on personal cards. We can work with that. Save every invoice, and we'll build a clean record from them at year-end.

The bigger risk is taxes. Once you're self-employed, nobody withholds for you. The IRS expects estimated payments during the year, and the road map tells you what each one should be.

If you're forming an LLC, it's also on the Texas franchise tax list from the start. Most new businesses owe nothing, but the annual report is still due May 15. We put it on the calendar the day the entity exists.

Questions Frisco Business Owners Ask

Do I need an S-corp right away?+

Usually not. The S-corp saves payroll tax only once there's enough profit to cover the extra cost of running payroll and a separate return. Plenty of clients start as an LLC and elect later in the year, once they can see what they're on pace to earn.

Do I still need to save receipts if I have a business bank account?+

Mostly no. With a dedicated account, the bank statement is your record. The exception is a large purchase that could look personal, such as a laptop. Keep that receipt. The IRS covers what to keep in its recordkeeping guidance.

Can you talk to my attorney while they draft the documents?+

Yes, and I'd rather be involved early. A simple org chart showing who owns what and where the money flows is usually enough for me to make sure the tax structure matches the legal one.

Do I have to come to the office?+

No. Our office is in Plano, and most Frisco clients meet us over video. In-person meetings are available by appointment.
