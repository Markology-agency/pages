Practice valuation

Why a CPA belongs in a transition

The bank checks whether you can pay the note. The broker prices the practice. Neither one is paid to tell you whether the profit is real, or what the deal does to your taxes for the next ten years.

We see the inside of hundreds of dental P&Ls. When a seller's lab fees sit far under what similar practices spend, we ask why before you sign. Sometimes there's a good answer. Sometimes the supplies are being paid by the office that isn't for sale.

Your practice is your biggest asset

Omar says this on almost every webinar, because owners forget it. If you raise the value of your practice by a meaningful amount, there's no other lever in your financial life, including the stock market, that moves your net worth the same way. That's true whether you're buying in, building it up, or getting ready to hand it off.

Questions we hear a lot

Do you help with the purchase price or just the taxes?

Both. We rebuild the seller's profit with a market-rate doctor's salary in it, so you can see what the practice earns as a business. Then we look at how the deal is structured for tax. Pricing the practice for sale is the broker's job, and we work alongside them.

Do you list practices for sale?

No. We don't run listings or act as a broker. If you're looking for practices, we're happy to look at any you find.

I'm years away from selling. Is it too early to talk?

It's the best time. The things that raise a practice's value, like clean books, a market salary for you, a sensible lease and steady collections, take a few years to show up in the numbers a buyer looks at.
