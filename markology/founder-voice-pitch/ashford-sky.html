Raw meeting transcript

Meeting: "Discovery, Mike Chen intro, DentalSoft diligence question"
Duration: 42 min · Attendees: darin@ashfordsky.com, mike@chencap.com
MIKE CHEN: Darin, thanks for making time. So we're in LOI on DentalSoft, it's a $4.8M revenue SaaS company doing dental practice management out of Provo. Seller's claiming $1.6M EBITDA. I need to know if those numbers are real before we go hard.
DARIN: First question I'd ask, what's their churn rate look like? Because in dental SaaS, anything over 1.5% monthly is a red flag that the ARR they're claiming isn't sticky. EBITDA looks great on paper, but if you're losing 20% of your customer base annually, that EBITDA is going to compress fast post-close.
MIKE: They said their churn is under 1%. Should I trust the data room?
DARIN: No. Never trust the data room. The data room is a sales document. What you want is the underlying export from their billing system, Stripe or wherever, and you build the cohort retention curves yourself. If you can't get raw billing data, that's your answer. They're hiding something or they don't know their numbers. Either way, no.
MIKE: OK. What about the EBITDA add-backs? Their QofE shows like $300K in owner add-backs.
DARIN: That's where most QofE reports lie by omission. Owner comp add-back is fine, but you have to replace it with what a hired CEO would actually cost. If the seller is taking $80K and a real CEO costs $200K, your real EBITDA add-back is $80K minus $200K, which is negative $120K. Most QofE reports just add the $80K back and call it done. That's how new owners get blindsided by year-two compression.
MIKE: Got it. We're looking at a 5.2x EBITDA multiple. Reasonable?
DARIN: For dental SaaS at that scale with verified retention, 4x to 6x is the band. 5.2x is fine if churn is really under 1.5%. If churn turns out to be 2-3%, you should be at 3x to 3.5x. Push back at LOI if the diligence reveals churn worse than what they claimed.

The Markology engine

Server-side filter
Decide whether the meeting is worth extracting from at all. Skip if all attendees are internal, duration is under 10 min, or the title matches a skip pattern (standup, payroll, 1:1, etc.). No AI cost on skipped meetings.
2
PII pre-scrub (regex)
Strip emails, phones, ZIPs, common first+last names, US states, major cities, and Ashford Sky's per-client block terms (target company names if known). First defensive pass before anything goes to Claude.
scrub("Mike Chen") → "[REDACTED:name]"
3
Claude extract
Read the transcript. Pull every distinct founder take, story, prospect pain point, FAQ, social-ready quote, and vocabulary entry. For Ashford Sky: aggressive deal-side anonymization in this pass.
4
Anonymization review
Second AI pass: catch anything regex missed. Target company name, specific deal sizes, geography, multiples too precise to identify a known deal. Jitter precise client-specific figures (~10%) so the original wouldn't recognize themselves. If an entry can't be cleaned without losing its point, it's dropped.
5
Router
Each entry filed into the right topic file with tagged metadata, plus the matching digest (faqs, social-ready, vocabulary). The library INDEX regenerates so the content-calendar skill can see fresh material.
