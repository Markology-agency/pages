why local

Why a Plano CPA and Not a National Tax Service

Texas has no personal income tax. People hear that and assume Texas taxes are simple. They aren't.

If your business is an LLC, even a single-member LLC, it's subject to the Texas franchise tax. The annual franchise tax report is due May 15. If you're under the no-tax-due threshold, you still file a Public Information Report or an Ownership Information Report.

If it's missed, the Comptroller can send a notice of forfeiture. A CPA here files it for clients every year, so it's already on the calendar.

The bigger reason is simpler. We're a local firm small enough that you always talk to the same person. I'm the owner, and I'm the one working with you. You won't be onboarded by one person and passed off to someone else.

Questions Plano Clients Ask

Does Texas have a state income tax?
−

Not on individuals. Texas has no personal income tax, so a Plano resident's return is federal only unless you earn in another state. Businesses are a different story, which is the next question.

What is the Texas franchise tax, and do I have to file it?
−

It's the state's tax on businesses that limit their owners' liability, such as LLCs and corporations. A sole proprietor with no LLC isn't a taxable entity. A single-member LLC is, even if the IRS treats it as a sole proprietorship (Texas Comptroller). Most small businesses owe no franchise tax. They still have to file the annual report by May 15.

Can you work with me if I earn in more than one state?
−

Yes. I have clients all over the country, and a lot of Plano clients have income from somewhere else. Locum shifts, telehealth patients, and rental properties are the usual sources. We file each state that applies and plan for it in the road map.

Will I be working with you or with staff?

Me. A few people help part-time with bookkeeping and basic prep. They won't really be emailing you. I'll be communicating with you directly.
