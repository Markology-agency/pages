the problem

The Second Income Nobody Withholds For

The side work pays well, and nothing is taken out of it. Your W-2 withholding doesn't know it exists. By April, the two have never met.

That's why the tax bill jumps in the first year of side income, even when the W-2 is the same as last year. The IRS taxes the household, not each paycheck separately.

The fix is to look at both incomes together, month by month, and decide how the tax on the side income gets paid.

One Household, One Plan

Withholding and estimates, set together

You can pay the tax on side income through quarterly estimated payments. You can also have your employer withhold more with a new Form W-4. Some clients use one, some use both. I'll show you which is simpler for your household.

The entity question, answered honestly

With a large W-2, an S-corp on the side income often isn't worth it. You've already paid in the Social Security you owe through the W-2. A second salary through your own S-corp starts that tax over again. We'll run the math before anyone files anything.

Deductions that belong to the side business

These include licensing, continuing education, a phone and laptop used for the work, and mileage where it's legitimate. A home office counts if it's a real one.

Retirement, without tripping over the W-2 plan

Side income can open up a solo 401(k). The limits interact with your employer's plan, though. We work out what goes where so nothing is over-funded.

questions

Questions Allen Professionals Ask

Can I just raise my W-2 withholding instead of making estimated payments?+

Yes, you absolutely can. The IRS says wage earners can avoid estimated tax by having more withheld. In December I look at the final paystubs for both spouses so we know exactly what's been withheld before the year closes.

Should I set up an S-corp for my consulting income?+

Usually not while you're earning a large W-2. The S-corp's benefit is lower self-employment tax. Once your W-2 has covered Social Security for the year, most of that benefit is already gone. If the side income grows into the main income, we revisit it.

Does my spouse's income change the plan?+

Yes. A joint return combines both of you, so the road map includes your spouse's pay, bonuses, and any business income too. It's included in the engagement because it's the same return.

Do I need an LLC for side income?+

Not for federal tax purposes. A single-member LLC reports the same income as a sole proprietor. In Texas, though, an LLC is subject to the franchise tax, so it adds an annual report. A separate business bank account matters more either way, because it keeps a clean record if the IRS ever asks. The LLC itself is a legal decision to run past your attorney.
