Cherry Hill AdvisoryInternal Audit · Risk · AI GovernanceFri · 🌐
Every internal audit function we speak with gets the same question from its board: are we safe with AI? It is the right question, and most organizations cannot yet answer it with evidence. That gap is why we wrote this. Auditing the AI Attack Surface is a Cherry Hill field brief covering thirteen ways attackers break or abuse AI, what each attack defeats, and where internal audit and the audit committee should press. Eight are high severity. All of them are mapped to the controls that hold. Most of these attacks are not new in concept. Phishing, impersonation, data theft and injection have existed for decades. What AI changes is the economics and the mechanics: the cost to run them collapses, the speed and scale rise sharply, and a new class of agentic attacks lets a single compromise produce real-world loss with no human in the loop. A crafted email the recipient never opened can cause an enterprise assistant to leak data. A familiar face on a video call can no longer be treated as proof of identity. These are documented losses from the last twenty-four months, not hypotheticals. The good news is buried in it. The controls that matter are rarely the exotic model-internals work people assume. They are governance basics applied to a new object: know where AI sits in your decision chains, scope hard what an agent is allowed to touch, verify high-value instructions through a second channel, and never let one system trust another system's AI output unchecked. Those are audit questions. The brief is built to help you ask them, and it closes with the TRUST AI Method, the methodology we use to assess AI programs against them.
