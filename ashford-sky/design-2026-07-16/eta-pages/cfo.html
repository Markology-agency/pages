The year-one reality

Revenue looks fine. So why does cash feel so tight?

Almost every buyer hits this in the first six months: the P&L matches the model, but the bank account doesn't. Debt service, working capital swings, and seller-era habits eat cash in ways a backward-looking report never shows. A CFO's job is to see it coming 13 weeks out.

FAQs

CFO questions from owner-operators

How is this different from accounting or bookkeeping?

Bookkeeping records what happened; accounting organizes and reports it. CFO work uses that foundation to answer forward-looking questions: can I afford this hire, what happens if revenue slows two months, how do I structure the next acquisition? It looks forward, and that's where the strategic value lives.

Do I need CFO support in year one, or is that overkill?

Year one is when it earns its keep: you're servicing debt, learning the business, and making decisions with the least history to go on. Some owners start monthly and move to quarterly once the business runs predictably. We recommend the cadence you need, not the most expensive one.

What does a fractional CFO cost vs a full-time hire?

A full-time CFO typically runs $150,000 to $250,000 a year before benefits. Fractional support delivers the same level of thinking scaled to a $500K-$5M business. Most buyers at this size need CFO-level thinking, not CFO-level headcount.

Can you help me plan the next acquisition or an exit?

Yes, that's the "beyond" part. We've been the buyer and the seller. When you're ready for deal two or preparing to exit, the diligence, structuring, and clean history are already in-house.
