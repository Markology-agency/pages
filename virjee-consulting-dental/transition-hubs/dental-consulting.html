STAGE TWO

Buying a practice

NEXT STEPBuying a PracticeDue diligence, financing and purchase structure before you sign.
Dental Monthly CFO Services →
Where you are

Buying a practice is the biggest financial decision most dentists make, and the numbers the seller shows you are rarely the numbers you'll live with. A seller's spouse running the front office for a token wage makes the profit look great on paper. The day you hire an office manager at a real wage, that profit drops.

What we do
Rebuild the seller's numbers the way you'll actually run the practice: three years of returns and P&Ls, overhead, production by provider, patient retention
Put a market-rate doctor's salary in place of whatever the seller paid themselves
Prepare the lender package and help you compare offers from dental-specific lenders
Plan the purchase structure and price split, because it affects your taxes for years after closing
Set up your first year as an owner: books, payroll, entity and owner salary, before the first return
From our client work

A doctor buying a practice got to sit with the seller and look at the schedule. It was completely full and booked out for weeks. Then we pulled the P&L, and the collections weren't there. A full schedule doesn't mean the money is being collected.

Busy but not profitable

Dental CFO Services →
Where you are

This is the most common call we get. Collections are good, the schedule is full, you're working hard and the take-home still isn't there. Every problem in a practice shows up in one of five numbers: collections, staff cost, variable costs, fixed costs and the profit left at the end. Your P&L works like an X-ray. Once you can read it, you know where to treat.

What we do
Read your five numbers and show you which one is the problem
Track the numbers that drive profit: overhead as a % of collections, revenue per patient, hygiene reactivation, case acceptance and cost per new patient
Compare you to practices at your stage, not to an average that fits nobody
Find the small moves, a fee here, a supply line there, that add up to a steady margin
From our client work

An established practice's supply costs dropped. They weren't buying less. They'd been overpaying the same vendor for a year and a half, because they were busy growing and the rep brought cookies every six months. We set a bonus for the front desk tied to finding a better price. Nothing else changed, and the savings went straight to profit.

Where you are

The best time to plan a sale is years before it, while there's still time to change the number. We start with three figures: what the practice is worth today, what you owe, and what you need to retire on. If the gap doesn't work, we know which levers to pull and how long it will take.

What we do
Clean up the two things that raise a price most: consistent bookkeeping and a market-rate owner salary
Plan the price split and how you're paid before the terms are set
Work beside your broker and attorney on the financial side
Stay with you through closing and your last return as an owner
What we see

Buyers come to us with seller P&Ls that are a mess, and the sad part is that clean books would have made those practices worth a lot more.
