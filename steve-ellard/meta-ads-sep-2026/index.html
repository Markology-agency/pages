Why we're running this now

Google Ads is the only paid channel on this account and it's carrying the whole funnel at $15/day against a footprint of two counties. The July diagnosis was that non-brand demand exists but the money terms sit on page two organically, so paid has to do the work. Meta adds the half of the market that isn't searching yet: the Cape business owner who knows their books got away from them over the summer and hasn't gone looking for anyone.

September is the right month for it. The season's over, the summer crews are winding down, and the planning window that actually changes an April tax bill runs from now to December 31. Those are real reasons to be in front of someone, not manufactured ones.

Deliberately not running a September 15 deadline ad. A dated deadline creative burns a week of learning, dies on September 16, and puts us in front of people at the point where they've already either paid or missed it. The two service problems below are live all month and all quarter.

Geo: mirror the Google Ads footprint exactly

Barnstable County MA + Plymouth County MA, plus Carver, Marion, Wareham and Bourne. Radius targeting is wrong here because the Cape's geography makes a radius spill into water and into Rhode Island. Use county and town entities, set to people living in this location, never "recently in".

Excluded: Bristol County and Norfolk County. Both were removed from Google in the July 30 restructure after they pulled Fall River, New Bedford, Taunton, Attleboro, Dedham, Quincy and Randolph traffic that never converted. Do not let Meta re-introduce them.

Detailed targeting

Start broad. The whole targetable population inside that footprint is small enough that layering interests will choke delivery.

  • Layer one (launch with this): no detailed targeting. Geo + age only.
  • Layer two (only if delivery is fine and cost per lead is high at day 10): narrow to

behaviours Small business owners, and job-title interests around business ownership.

Do not add income, homeowner, or life-event targeting. It doesn't help a B2B service and it shrinks an already-small pool.

Brand identity: this campaign runs two names

The payroll ad runs as Atlantic Payroll, the other two run as Steve Ellard CPA. That split matches how the business already presents itself: the payroll page is titled "Cape Cod Payroll Services | Atlantic Payroll by Ellard CPA", so payroll has its own name in market while the tax and advisory work sits under Steve's.

On Meta the page identity is set per ad creative, so all three can live in one ad set. At this budget that's the right call, because splitting into two ad sets starves both before either learns anything.

DECISION / blocker: does Atlantic Payroll have its own Facebook Page? If yes, we need admin access to it to run the payroll ad from that identity. If no, the payroll ad runs from the Steve Ellard CPA page with Atlantic Payroll named in the copy and on the creative, which is a reasonable fallback and still reads correctly given the "by Ellard CPA" lockup already in use.

Creative: three concepts

Voice rules as everywhere else for this client. Conversational, dry, a little New England. Always "on Cape Cod", never "in Cape Cod". No guarantees about outcomes or savings, which matters most in concept 2, where the whole idea sits near a savings claim and has to stay on the right side of it.

Deliberately not running a September 15 deadline ad. A dated creative burns a week of learning, dies on September 16, and reaches people at the point where they've already paid or already missed it. All three below are live all quarter.

Concept 1: Payroll and the seasonal headcount swing

Brand identity: Atlantic Payroll.

The concept the ad account is named for, and the cleanest local hook of the three, because the headcount swing is specific to how businesses here actually operate.

Primary text

You hired twelve people in June and you're down to three. Somewhere in that stretch someone

moved, a couple of people got paid as contractors because it was quicker, and the paperwork

got handled fast because the season was starting and there wasn't time for it.

>

Atlantic Payroll runs payroll for Cape businesses year round, which mostly means we're the

ones keeping track of that instead of you. Seasonal crews, headcount that changes every month,

and the Massachusetts rules about who counts as an employee rather than a contractor. It's the

part of a business that gets away from people quickest, and it's a straightforward one to hand

over.

Headline: Payroll that keeps up with your season Description: Atlantic Payroll. Cape Cod payroll, year round. CTA button: Learn More Website destination: https://www.stevenmellardcpa.com/payroll-services-cape-cod/

Concept 2: What actually changes an April tax bill

Brand identity: Steve Ellard CPA.

Whitney's angle: the thing that makes the difference to an April tax bill is year-round planning, not the person typing up the return in March. The switch-accountants ask is the point of the ad.

Careful with this one. The idea lives right next to a savings promise, and we can't make one. The claim below is that planning happens during the year and a return filed in spring can only report what already happened. That's accurate and it isn't a guarantee. Do not let it drift into "pay less", a dollar figure, or a percentage.

Primary text

By the time your return gets filed in the spring, the year is already over. Whatever it says

is a record of decisions that were made months earlier, and there isn't much anyone can do

about it at that point.

>

That's the real difference between accountants. Not who fills the forms in faster, but whether

anyone was looking at your numbers in September and October, while the decisions were still

open. If April keeps arriving as a surprise, that's usually what's missing.

>

We work with Cape businesses year round, and this is the stretch of the year when that work

actually happens.

Headline: A surprise in April starts in September Description: Year-round tax planning for Cape Cod businesses. CTA button: Book Now Website destination: https://www.stevenmellardcpa.com/contact/

Headline: The quiet season is planning season Description: Year-round support for Cape Cod businesses. CTA button: Book Now Website destination: https://www.stevenmellardcpa.com/contact/
