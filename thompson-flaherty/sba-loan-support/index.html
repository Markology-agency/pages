Where SBA applications usually get stuck.

Most of the small business owners who come to us mid-application are stuck for the same handful of reasons. The work itself is not complicated for someone who has done it before. The problem is that almost no one has done it before, until they have.

Pain 01

Underwriters want CPA-prepared statements, not a QuickBooks export.

Lenders need financials with a CPA's name on them, prepared to a recognized standard. A PDF straight out of QuickBooks looks fine to you, but it does not check the box the underwriter is looking at.

Pain 02

Cash flow projections come back with red marks.

Banks have a format they expect. Twelve or twenty-four months out, broken down by month, with assumptions documented underneath. If the projection looks improvised or the numbers do not tie back to your last twelve months, the file slows down.

Pain 03

Three years of tax returns have to reconcile to your books.

Underwriters cross-check your tax returns against the financial statements you submit. Small inconsistencies (depreciation method, owner draws labeled wrong, a missing schedule) read as red flags, even when nothing is actually wrong.

Pain 04

The clock is running while you are still running the business.

Most SBA applications come with a real deadline behind them: a property under contract, an acquisition closing, equipment that needs to be ordered. Doing the financial prep yourself on nights and weekends usually means missing the timeline or sending in something half-finished.

What we put together for you.

Every SBA loan is a little different, so we tailor the package to your loan type, your lender, and your timeline. The three pieces below are the core of almost every package we deliver.

01 · Financial statements

CPA-prepared financials that satisfy the underwriter on the first pass.

Compiled, reviewed, or audited based on what your lender requires. Balance sheet, income statement, and cash flow statement, all prepared under our firm's letter so the underwriter sees a CPA signature where they expect one. If your books need cleanup before we can issue the statements, we handle that as part of the engagement.

02 · Cash flow projections

Projections built to lender standards, not invented in a spreadsheet.

Twelve-month or three-year projections in the format SBA underwriters expect, with monthly breakdowns, ratio analysis where lenders look for it, and a documented assumption sheet underneath every line. The numbers tie back to your historical financials so an underwriter can trace the logic.

03 · Tax return package

Three years of returns reconciled to your financials, line by line.

We pull the last three years of business and personal returns, reconcile them against the financial statements going into the package, and flag anything that would slow the file down (book-to-tax differences, missing schedules, owner compensation labeled the wrong way). When something needs amending, we do the amendment as part of the same engagement.

Why it matters that a CPA prepares this.

SBA underwriting is a paper exercise. The underwriter does not know you, has not visited your business, and is mostly looking at the documents in front of them to decide whether the loan is safe to make. Documents that look right, tie back, and carry a CPA signature land in a different pile than documents that do not.
