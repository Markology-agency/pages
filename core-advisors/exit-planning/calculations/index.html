2. Seller's discretionary earnings

SDE = collections − (staff × 12) − (opex × 12) + personal add-backs

Owner's compensation is not added back, because it was never subtracted. Question 3 is explicitly "everyone but you", and question 4 is facilities and supplies, so the owner's pay is already inside the figure. Adding it again would inflate SDE by the owner's full comp.

Worked example

LineAmount
Collections$1,200,000
Staff, $35,000/mo−$420,000
Operating expenses, $18,000/mo−$216,000
Personal add-backs+$24,000
SDE$588,000

We still ask question 2. It sets up the add-back idea, and it is stored with the lead so you can see what the owner is paying themselves before the call. It does not enter the arithmetic.

The 72% line is the Exit Planning Institute's Attractiveness Index threshold, and the page says so on screen. The dentist also sees a short write-up of whichever drivers scored below 3, ordered worst first.

4. The two numbers we need from you

The result screen shows two figures and the gap between them:

Value today            = SDE × MULT_TODAY
Best-in-class potential = SDE × MULT_BIC
The gap                 = SDE × (MULT_BIC − MULT_TODAY)

Both are multiples of SDE, not EBITDA. Dental EBITDA multiples of 3x to 7x would overstate this output roughly threefold, so they are ruled out in the code and in our notes.

Our working assumption is a floor of 1.0x and a ceiling of 2.0x, which came from the campaign work, not from you. Until you confirm, the page shows a red placeholder instead of a dollar figure, so no dentist is quoted a number you haven't signed off. On the worked example above, that range would read:

MultipleShown to a dentist with $588,000 SDE

2.0x$1,176,000

Two questions, then we can turn it on:

  1. Are 1.0x and 2.0x the right floor and ceiling for a dental practice at this SDE?
  2. Should the "today" multiple flex with the transferability score, so a 30% score prices lower than an 80% score, or do you want one flat multiple for everyone until we have real comps?

5. What the page never does

  • No guarantee of a sale price, and the disclaimer says so twice.
  • No formal valuation or appraisal claim.
  • No industry statistic applied to dentists that was measured on business owners generally. Where an EPI figure is quoted, the page says it is for business owners generally.
