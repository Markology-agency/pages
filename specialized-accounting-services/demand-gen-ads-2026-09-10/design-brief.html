Read this first: who is actually seeing these

These ads are not aimed at the general public and not at people shopping for an accountant. They go to a list of existing franchise operators, uploaded as a Customer Match audience, plus people Google finds who look like them.

So the viewer already runs the business. They know what a P&L is. They are not being taught what accounting is, they are being shown that somebody understands their specific operation. Creative that explains accounting to them will read as condescending.

They will see this mid-scroll, on a phone, in a YouTube feed or the Gmail Promotions tab. One idea per frame. If it needs a second read it has failed.

The one trap on concept 1

SAS serves two different Ace audiences and they are not interchangeable. Concept 1 is Ace Hardware, the retail stores. It is not Ace Handyman Services.

Concept 1 should look like a hardware store: aisles, stock, a service counter. No vans, no tool belts, no handyman-at-your-door imagery. A draft was rejected in August 2026 for mixing exactly these two up.

Home-services imagery belongs on concept 4 and nowhere else.

Photography

This is the decision that will make or break the set.

Real environment photography will beat anything typographic here, because the whole idea is "we know your specific business". A hardware aisle, a coffee bar at 6am, a studio between classes, a van at a job site.

Recommended: licensed stock, chosen for the room rather than the people. Shot from an owner's eye level, not a customer's. Warm and real, not glossy corporate.

Avoid: the standard accounting stock vocabulary. No calculators, no stacks of coins, no handshakes, no suited people pointing at a laptop, no floating charts. If the photo would work for any accounting firm anywhere, it is the wrong photo.

If no photo budget is approved, go typographic with a strong blue ground and let the type do the work. Say which route you're taking before you build all twelve.

Hard don'ts

  1. No brand names or brand logos on the creative. Not Ace Hardware, Biggby Coffee, Club Pilates, One Hour, Benjamin Franklin, Mister Sparky or DRYmedic. Not their colours or their marks either. Using another company's trademark in ad creative gets the ads pulled, and SAS doing their accounting is not the same as permission to advertise on their brand. The targeting handles who sees it, the creative stays SAS-branded.
  2. No numbers. No percentages, no dollar figures, no "save X". SAS content rules ban unsourced benchmarks, and ad creative can't promise a financial outcome.
  3. No payroll. SAS does not do payroll. Nothing in the imagery or copy should imply it.
  4. No fake dashboards or invented screenshots. If a screen appears in shot it has to be a real product or blurred past legibility.
  5. No em dashes in any typeset text. Commas, periods, colons.
