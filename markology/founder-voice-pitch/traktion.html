Raw Fireflies transcript

Meeting: "Discovery, Sarah Johnson intro call"
Duration: 27 min · Attendees: mebea@traktionaccounting.com, sarah@brightpaththerapy.com
SARAH JOHNSON: Hi Mebea, thanks for hopping on. So I'm a solo therapist over at Bright Path Therapy in Austin, doing about $184K this year, my CPA at Smith & Associates said wait til I hit $200K before electing S-corp but it doesn't feel right.
MEBEA: Yeah, that advice is way too conservative for most therapists. The way I think about it, once you're netting around $80K to $100K in profit, the S-corp math usually works. If you're at $184K revenue with decent margins, you're at like $130K profit, well past that threshold. The mechanic is straightforward, you split income between W-2 salary and distributions, and only the salary portion gets self-employment tax. For someone at your level we usually see meaningful annual SE tax savings.
SARAH: Wait so does that mean I still have to do estimated taxes if I'm an S-corp? My friend Jen over in Round Rock said she didn't have to anymore.
MEBEA: Common confusion. Payroll handles your salary tax, withholding is done there. But your distributions, no taxes withheld. IRS still expects quarterly estimated payments to cover the tax on the distribution portion. Payroll handles your salary. Estimated taxes handle your distributions. You need both.
SARAH: OK that makes sense. One more thing, I might bring on an associate next year, does any of this change?
MEBEA: Yeah it changes the math. You'd want strong collected revenue from that associate before the hire is profitable, at a healthy 20-30% margin. We call that the ramp period. Plan for about six months before they hit break-even.

Claude extract

4
Anonymization review
Second AI pass: catch anything the regex missed. First-name-only mentions, distinctive business descriptors, neighborhood names, identifying detail combinations. If an entry can't be cleaned without losing meaning, it's dropped.
5
Router
