Why we're running this now

Google Ads is the only paid channel on this account and it's carrying the whole funnel at $15/day against a footprint of two counties. The July diagnosis was that non-brand demand exists but the money terms sit on page two organically, so paid has to do the work. Meta adds the half of the market that isn't searching yet: the Cape business owner who knows their books got away from them over the summer and hasn't gone looking for anyone.

September is the right month for it. The season's over, the Q3 estimated payment lands September 15, and the planning window that actually changes an April tax bill runs from now to December 31. That's a real reason to be in front of someone, not a manufactured one.

Geo: mirror the Google Ads footprint exactly

Barnstable County MA + Plymouth County MA, plus Carver, Marion, Wareham and Bourne. Radius targeting is wrong here because the Cape's geography makes a radius spill into water and into Rhode Island. Use county and town entities, set to people living in this location, never "recently in".

Excluded: Bristol County and Norfolk County. Both were removed from Google in the July 30 restructure after they pulled Fall River, New Bedford, Taunton, Attleboro, Dedham, Quincy and Randolph traffic that never converted. Do not let Meta re-introduce them.

Detailed targeting

Start broad. The whole targetable population inside that footprint is small enough that layering interests will choke delivery.

  • Layer one (launch with this): no detailed targeting. Geo + age only.
  • Layer two (only if delivery is fine and cost per lead is high at day 10): narrow to

behaviours Small business owners, and job-title interests around business ownership.

Do not add income, homeowner, or life-event targeting. It doesn't help a B2B service and it shrinks an already-small pool.

Destination

DECISION: website vs instant form.

Recommended: instant lead form. The site's contact page converts, but Meta traffic is colder than search traffic and asking a Cape business owner to leave Facebook, load a site and fill in a form is where this loses people. An instant form keeps them in-app.

Form spec if we go that way:

  • Form name: SE - Sep 2026 - Book a Call
  • Type: more volume
  • Intro headline: Talk to a Cape Cod CPA before September 15
  • Fields: first name, last name, email, phone. Nothing else.
  • One qualifying question: What do you need help with? with options: Business taxes,

Personal taxes, Payroll, Bookkeeping, Not sure yet

  • Privacy policy: https://www.stevenmellardcpa.com/privacy-policy/ (confirm this URL resolves

before build; Meta rejects a form with a dead privacy link)

  • Thank-you screen: We'll be in touch within one business day. Button to

https://www.stevenmellardcpa.com/contact/

If we go website instead, destination is https://www.stevenmellardcpa.com/contact/ with ?utm_source=fb&utm_medium=paid_social&utm_campaign=se_leads_sep2026. One URL covers both platforms, since Meta swaps fb and ig per placement automatically.

Lead routing: instant-form leads sit inside Meta until someone pulls them. Before launch, either connect the form to Steve's inbox through Zapier, or put a recurring pull on our side. A lead form nobody empties is worse than no lead form.

Tracking: verify before spend

  1. Confirm a Meta pixel exists on stevenmellardcpa.com and is firing. The account has never run

Meta, so assume it doesn't until seen.

  1. If no pixel: install one, or accept LEAD_GENERATION optimization on instant forms, which

doesn't need site tracking.

Creative: three concepts

Same voice rules as everything else for this client. Conversational, dry, a little New England. Always "on Cape Cod", never "in Cape Cod". No guarantees about outcomes or savings.

Copy direction carries over from the July 30 Google work: lead on local, proactive, responsive. "Tired of chasing your accountant?" came from a real July enquiry and it's the single best line we have for this client, so concept 1 builds on it.

Concept 1: Tired of chasing your accountant

Primary text

Most people who call us in September start the same way. They say they've got an accountant,

they just can't get hold of them, and they've stopped trying.

>

We're on the Cape, we answer, and we'd rather talk to you now than in April when the options

have run out. If your books got away from you over the season, that's a normal September

problem and it's a fixable one.

Headline: A Cape Cod CPA who answers the phone Description: Local, proactive and responsive. Talk to us. CTA button: Book Now

Concept 2: September 15 deadline

Primary text

Your Q3 estimated tax payment is due September 15, and it covers what you earned from June

through August.

>

For most Cape businesses that's the biggest stretch of the year, which makes it the payment

most likely to come up short. Paying what you paid in April and hoping it evens out usually

doesn't even out. If you'd like someone to check where you actually stand before you send it,

there's still time this week.

Headline: September 15 is closer than it looks Description: Cape Cod CPA. We'll look at the numbers with you. CTA button: Learn More

Time-boxed. Pause this ad on September 16 and let concepts 1 and 3 carry the rest of the month.

Concept 3: Off-season cleanup

Primary text

The season's over and the Cape is quiet again, which makes the next few weeks the only stretch

of the year when a business owner here has both the numbers and the time to look at them.

>

Equipment, retirement contributions, whether your business structure still fits what the

business has turned into. Those decisions are live right now and mostly closed by January. We

work with year-round and seasonal businesses across the Cape and the South Shore, and this is

the conversation we'd rather have in October than in April.

Headline: The planning window is open until December 31 Description: Year-round support for Cape Cod businesses. CTA button: Book Now

What success looks like at 30 days

Honest framing, because this account has been burned by vanity conversions before. At $8 to $15/day into a footprint this size, a realistic month one is a handful of genuine enquiries, not a pipeline. The number that matters is how many of them Steve would recognise as a real prospect, checked by asking him, not by reading the Meta dashboard.
