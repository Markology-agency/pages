Add back smell test (7 line items reviewed)

The seller's memo asks you to add these back to EBITDA to justify the price. Not all of them will hold up when a lender or a diligence CPA looks. Here is our take, item by item.

  • Strip
    Consulting fees, owner's brother ($18,000)
    Related party. Unless there is an invoice trail and a service the practice actually needed, this is a distribution wearing a costume. Lenders will not credit it as an add back.
    $18,000
  • Strip
    Family health insurance ($18,200)
    The practice will still need to insure the owner (which is now you) and, in most cases, offer coverage to eligible staff. It is not going away just because ownership changes. Do not add this back.
    $18,200
  • Strip
    Owner CE trip, Bahamas Study Club ($8,400)
    You will need CE too. Some travel is legitimate, but the destination pattern suggests personal use dressed as CE. At best, add back a portion.
    $8,400
  • Negotiate
    Owner personal travel coded to practice ($4,800)
    Depends on documentation. Ask for the expense reports. If it is not documented as business travel, treat it as a distribution.
    $4,800

    Some referral and staff meals are real, but $9,800 is heavy for a solo practice. Ask for detail and expect to add back only 30% to 40%.

3 questions to ask the seller before you sign the LOI

These come out of the red flags above. If the answers are not clear, that is data too.

  1. Why is non owner payroll running 50%+ of collections?
    Payroll is the biggest lever on future profit and the biggest place to hide bloat. You need to know whether it is genuine overstaffing (you can fix it) or an underproducing associate (harder), before you underwrite any of it as savings.
  2. Can we see the lab invoices for the last 6 months, not just the summary?
    Lab at 12% is either a case mix that will not repeat under you, a bad contract, or unbilled remakes. All three change the price. The invoices tell you which one.
  3. What share of production came from patients the owner treated personally, and what is the plan for those charts at transition?
    At 76% owner production, retention of those patients is the deal. Ask for the transition plan in writing. A short overlap and a warm intro protocol move numbers by tens of thousands.
