Food cost at 28% because that's the goal. Labor at 30% because anything higher feels uncomfortable. That's how most restaurant budgets get built. A revenue number goes in first, and every cost line gets written to make it work. Then the kitchen runs at 32% and labor lands at 35%, and the budget is wrong before the first quarter closes. A budget built on targets instead of actuals isn't a plan. It's a wish list. The fix is to start where the money really goes: your prime cost, as it runs today. Then decide what you'll change and budget the change. That's the first thing Tanya covers at October's Deep Dive Training, Tuesday, October 20, 1:00 PM PT on Zoom. If your 2027 labor budget is built from wages, it's already short. Wages are what the POS labor report shows. They aren't what labor costs you. Add the employer share of payroll taxes, workers' comp and whatever benefits you carry, and the real number is several points higher than the one you planned against. Budget labor fully loaded, and budget it by week against the covers you expect. Then the labor line in your budget matches the one in your bank account. I co-own eight restaurants, and I lead a team that builds budgets for restaurant operators every day. The budgets that get used all year have one thing in common. They start from what the restaurant actually spends. Not from the revenue number the owner hopes for. The ones that end up in a drawer usually made the same few mistakes. Costs written to fit a sales goal. Labor budgeted as wages only. Twelve equal months for a business that is nothing like equal month to month. No cash plan for the slow season. None of that is hard to fix. It just has to be built in from the start. So on Tuesday, October 20, I'm teaching exactly how we build a restaurant budget, live, with Q&A at the end. Take your annual budget, divide by twelve, and you've built a plan that's wrong every single month. Restaurant sales don't come in evenly. Your slowest month and your busiest month aren't close. Spread the year flat and every month looks like a big beat or a big miss. Within a quarter, nobody trusts the budget anymore. Build it month by month instead. Use last year's actual monthly pattern, then layer in what's changing. Now a slow January is on plan, and a soft June is a real signal worth acting on. Tanya covers the month-by-month build at October's Deep Dive Training, Tuesday, October 20, 1:00 PM PT on Zoom.
