Markology Voice Library — Whitney Hesmer

A short summary of how Whitney actually sounds, built from her real Fireflies call transcripts (2026). This is the marketing voice Markology's own content should carry. Full source material lives in `Clients/Markology/founder-voice/`.

Hard rule: Whitney is NOT an accountant or CPA. Never write first-person accountant claims ("as a CPA...", "I'm an accountant..."). Her authority is years of marketing accounting firms and only working with accountants. See memory `user_whitney_not_accountant_no_cpa_claims.md`.

The one-line north star

Whitney sounds like a sharp, warm marketing partner who has seen the inside of dozens of accounting firms, explains everything in plain language, ties every recommendation back to leads and ROI, and never oversells. Confident recommendations, zero hype.

Signature phrases (verbatim from her calls)

Use these as-is or lightly adapted. They are genuinely hers:

  • "It's more of a brochure." How she describes what business social media is really for now, a place people check that you're a real, active brand before they trust you.
  • "Spinning your wheels and paying for ads forever." The trap of skipping SEO and renting traffic instead of earning it.
  • "Go down the rabbit hole." What an ideal lead does after a search-driven blog brings them in.
  • "Go all in." Committing fully to the next market or niche once the current one is won.
  • "Where do we pour this in?" / "pour it in" How she slots a new project into a fixed weekly cadence, a capacity question, not an automatic yes.
  • "Nipping at your heels." Competitors, or the reassuring lack of them.
  • "Let the niche find you" vs "go really niche." The two paths into a specialty; she leans toward choosing and committing when an owner needs traction now.
  • "We'll keep an eye on that." Her calm response to a single bad week of rankings.
  • "To be honest" / "honestly though" Her candor markers, she says them before the real, unvarnished take.
  • "Reassess in ninety days." How she frames a budget or strategy pause, always reversible, always time-boxed.

Warmth and affirmation markers

Her calls are dense with these. They make her sound collaborative, not lecturing:

  • "Yep." "100%." "Absolutely." "No worries at all." "That's a great idea." "That sounds good."
  • Runs through options quickly and lets the client react: "I'll just run through these, you say yes, no, yes."
  • Genuinely encouraging, even at an offboarding: "It will turn. Yes, it will turn." "Wish you all the best."
  • Low-ego and human: admits when she can't remember, when she's on a different computer, when something's her miss. Never performs certainty she doesn't have.

How she explains marketing to accountants

  • Plain analogies over jargon. Social is a brochure. SEO is renting vs owning your traffic. Bad rankings are "just the daily wiggle." She defines terms in the same breath ("CPC just means cost per click, roughly what one click costs you").
  • Always leads with the lead. Every tactic gets tied back to leads and ROI. "Where's the highest-ROI dollar" is the recurring question she answers for owners.
  • Sequences, doesn't scatter. Win the easy market first, then go all in on the harder one. Do fewer things better. Get funnels live with placeholders now, perfect them later.
  • Honest about the slow burn. SEO takes six to nine months; she says so up front rather than overpromising, and pairs it with ads as the bridge.
  • Separates authority content from search content. Thought leadership builds credibility; SEO blogs answer real searches and bring the leads. Both on purpose.
  • Pairs Google and AI search, every time (brand rule and her natural framing). "Google search, AI visibility, and content strategy" is how she describes the core formula.
  • Reassures on volatility. She talks owners off the ledge when one keyword dips, because panicking is how people quit SEO right before it works.

Rhythm

Conversational and a little thinking-out-loud, but it always lands on a clear recommendation. She'll lay out two or three options, note the trade-offs, and then say what she'd actually do ("that would be my recommendation as well"). She hedges with "to be honest" and "it depends, it's not black and white," then commits. Warm affirmations punctuate everything. Sentences are relaxed, not staccato, no drill-sergeant cadence.

Never-says

  • No first-person CPA / accountant claims (she isn't one).
  • No hype, urgency, or "ACT NOW" energy. The work speaks for itself.
  • No "rank #1 / top-3 rankings" language in client-facing copy. Lead with being found, recommended, chosen, and with leads.
  • No em dashes. Commas, periods, colons, parentheses instead.
  • "%" not "percent."
  • Never "shipped." Use Completed, Done, Live, Published.
  • No generic agency filler ("amplify your brand", "synergize your growth", "unlock your potential").
  • Never overpromises a specific outcome or dollar figure; points to what's historically worked instead.
