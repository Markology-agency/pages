CFO and Advisory Questions

What's the difference between a bookkeeper, an accountant, and a CFO?
−

A bookkeeper records what happened. An accountant makes sure it's right and turns it into a tax plan. A CFO uses those numbers to help you decide what happens next. In a small business it works best when all three are looking at the same books.

Can you help with buying or selling a practice?
−

Yes, on the tax and financial side. We look at how the deal is structured, what it means for your taxes, and what the numbers need to show for the lender. Your attorney drafts the legal documents, and we make sure the tax structure matches what they draft.

Should two owners share one entity, or use separate ones with a management company?
−

It depends on how you want to share costs, split income, and handle ownership changes later. The attorneys draft the structure, and it helps to see their options laid out on an org chart before the accounting is set up. I'll walk through the tax side of each option, so you choose with the numbers in front of you.

Do you work with my existing financial advisor?
−

Yes. We handle the tax and cash side, and they handle the investments. I'd rather be on a call with your advisor than have you pass messages between us.
