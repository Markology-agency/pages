The short version

You show up in Google for 1,797 different searches, and you are in the top three for 849 of them. That is a real footprint for a firm of your size, and it is built almost entirely on the therapist-specific content published over the past year.

On your core service searches you sit mostly on page one or two, with CFO for therapists as the standout at an average position of 2.9. Several others reached the top five during the last few months.

Total visits dipped over the summer. The bulk of that came from one article about therapist retirement ages, which had been drawing a large number of readers who were curious about the profession rather than looking for an accountant. Over the same period the pages that attract practice owners grew, which is the trade you want.

What is working

Entity and tax decision content. LLC versus PLLC, reasonable compensation, S-corp for group practices and the California entity guide are all growing. These answer a question a practice owner is actively trying to settle, which is why they hold their positions.

Comparison content. Your reviews page more than doubled its traffic and moved from position 10 to 6. Therapists comparing providers are finding you.

State-specific guidance. The California article went from nothing to page one in a single quarter, which suggests the same approach would travel to other states.

How this is measured

Position is your average place in Google's results across every time you appeared. An average of 8 means you typically sit around eighth on the first page.

Times shown counts how often a Traktion page appeared in someone's results, whether or not they clicked.

Visits are clicks through to the site from an unpaid Google result.

Figures run to 30 August. Google reports with a two to three day delay, so the final August numbers will settle slightly higher. Comparisons use three full months on each side so that a single busy week does not distort the picture.
