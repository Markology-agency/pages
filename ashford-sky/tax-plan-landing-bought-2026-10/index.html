Why the first year is the one that matters

If you bought a business this year, the purchase itself creates tax decisions that you don't face in a normal year. Several of them are much harder to change once your first return as the owner is filed.

Several choices on your first return are hard or costly to undo later, so it pays to get them right the first time.

  1. The deal is still open on paper

    How the purchase price is split, how your acquisition debt is reported and whether your entity still fits all show up on your first return. Some of these can be changed later, but usually with amended returns and extra cost.

  2. Your first-year deductions are the biggest you'll see

    Equipment, vehicles and build-out often make up a big part of what you paid. The elections you make decide whether you write them off this year or over many years. Most deal costs get added to what you paid for the business and are written off over time, so how they're split matters too.

  3. The calendar runs out on December 31

    Equipment purchases and the salary you pay yourself have to happen before December 31. Some retirement and entity choices have a little more time, but planning now keeps all of them open.

What we look at

Here are the areas we review in your plan.

Purchase price allocation

If you bought the assets, how the price was split between them shapes your write-offs. If your purchase agreement didn't set the split, there may still be room. If it did, we plan the write-offs that follow from it.

Entity election

We check whether the structure you bought into, or set up to buy, still fits now that you own the business.

How you pay yourself

We look at salary, distributions or both. The IRS expects a reasonable salary, and the mix also affects your lender's covenants.

Equipment, vehicles, build-out

We review what came with the business, what you've bought since, and the elections that decide when you write them off.

Acquisition debt

We look at the interest, the loan costs and how your SBA loan or seller note is reported on the return.

Estimated payments

If you pay in at least what you owed last year, or a bit more at higher incomes, you generally avoid an underpayment penalty in April.
