Selling a dental practice in the next two years

You've decided. Maybe it's six years, maybe it's twenty-six. Before a broker puts a number on it, know your own number and what the sale will look like after tax.

Book a call with the Virjee Consulting team

What we do before you list

We value the practice the way a careful buyer will. That means a market-rate doctor's salary in place of whatever you've been paying yourself, one-off expenses added back, and anything the buyer will have to add in, like an office manager at a real wage.

Then we show you the gap. Here's what the practice is worth, here's what you owe, and here's what you'd walk away with. If that number doesn't get you where you want to go, it's better to know now while you still have time to change it.

The tax side of the sale

How the price is split between goodwill, equipment and other assets changes what you owe, and the buyer usually wants the split that's best for them. How you take payment matters too. We plan this with you before the LOI, while there's still room to negotiate.

Who we work with

You'll have a broker and an attorney. We sit beside them on the financial side, from the valuation through closing and your final tax return as an owner.

Questions we hear a lot

How is a dental practice valued for sale?

Usually off profit, after adjusting the owner's pay to market and adding back one-off costs. You'll also hear a percentage of collections quoted. We look at both and explain where your practice sits.

Do I pay tax when I sell my dental practice?

Usually, yes. How much, and when, depends on how the price is allocated and how you're paid. That's why we plan it before the deal terms are set.

Can you help if I'm selling to my associate?

Yes. Internal sales need the same valuation and tax planning, plus a payment structure your associate can actually afford.
