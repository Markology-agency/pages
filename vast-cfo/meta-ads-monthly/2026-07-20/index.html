Meta Ads Report

VAST CFO

Over the last 30 days, $537.80 on Meta produced 8 lead form submissions from restaurant operators, at an average of $67.22 each. The money was not spread evenly. One campaign generated every lead in July at $34.14 apiece, while an awareness campaign spent $227.60 and generated none. The recommendation for the next 30 days is to put the whole budget behind the campaign that is converting.

Half the leads came from the 65+ bracket, which is worth a second look rather than a celebration. On a lead form campaign, an older skew combined with vague form answers is often a sign of accidental taps rather than genuine interest. The 45 to 64 range, which is where multi-unit operators tend to sit, is currently the most expensive place to buy a lead. Response was evenly split between men and women (4 leads each).

Lead Quality

Meta recorded 8 form submissions. Looking at what people actually typed in, they fall into two groups.

Source campaignLeadsWhat they gave us

June On-Demand Webinar5One named an identifiable restaurant. The other four left the website field as filler text, and two of those arrived through Audience Network, a placement where accidental taps are common.

This changes the read on cost. The June webinar campaign looks cheaper per lead at $41.56, but only one of its five leads is clearly a restaurant operator. The July campaign's $34.14 leads are all verifiable businesses. On a like-for-like basis, the campaign currently running is the better buy, and the gap is bigger than the headline numbers suggest.

What To Do Next

1

Increase the budget on the campaign that is converting

Do this first The Leads campaign has generated 3 verifiable restaurant leads at $34.14 each while spending an average of $7.32 a day. That is the cheapest and cleanest lead source in the account, and it is the one being starved. Raising it in stages and watching whether the cost per lead holds is the single highest-value move available.

2

Do not restart the awareness campaign in its current form

Stop the leak It spent $227.60, reached 55,934 people, and produced no leads and no follow-up. Broad awareness only pays off when there is a retargeting campaign behind it to re-approach the people who paid attention. Until that second step exists, that budget does more work inside the lead campaign.

3

Build the retargeting layer, then awareness can earn its place

Compound the spend Two audiences worth creating: people who opened the lead form but did not submit it, and people who engaged with a Vast CFO post in the last 30 days. A small retargeting campaign pointed at those two groups reaches people who already know the name, which is normally where the cheapest booked calls come from.

4

Tighten placements and test Stories deliberately

Quality control Audience Network produced two of the weakest-quality leads in the period. Facebook Stories produced three leads at $30.37 on a small slice of budget. Splitting these out so they are chosen rather than automatic will give a much clearer read next month.

5

Add a second creative against the winner

Protect the result Everything currently working rests on one ad. Running a second concept alongside it protects against fatigue and shows whether the result comes from the message or from the campaign setup.
