THE SHORT VERSION

Four findings that decide this.

1. The money is in the umbrella, not the trades. "Construction accounting," "contractor bookkeeping," "job costing," "WIP reports," "retainage" and their variants are worth about 28,850 searches a month between them. That is roughly two thirds of everything available, and it is a single content build rather than thirty. Landscaping, the largest individual trade, is 3,980.

2. Most trades have no accounting-search market at all. Thirty-one verticals returned either nothing or single-digit volumes. Drywall, siding, insulation, excavation, fencing, septic, locksmith, welding, solar and EV chargers are all empty. Some verticals look busy until you read the intent: fencing has around 500 searches a month, but every one of them is a homeowner asking whether a new fence is deductible. That is the wrong buyer.

3. Two SERP patterns repeat in every single vertical. Any term containing "accounting software" belongs to field-service SaaS (ServiceTitan, Jobber, FreshBooks, Housecall Pro, Deltek) and cannot be won by an accounting firm. Any term shaped "bookkeeping for X" or "X tax deductions" sits at difficulty 0 to 18 and is currently held by offshore bookkeeping mills and content farms with no CPA credential behind them. That second group is the entire opportunity.

4. Where actual CPA firms rank, the difficulty collapses. cpa for contractors is difficulty 7 with a $10 click cost, and page one is nothing but independent firms. construction accounting services is difficulty 16 at a $34 click cost, page one is nine CPA firms. These are the two terms that prove a firm can win here, because firms already are.

The decision this data supports

Go national on the contractor and construction umbrella, with four or five trade spokes underneath it. Do not attempt a page per trade. The trade-specific layer is worth 16,830 searches a month spread across twenty-one viable verticals, and more than half of that sits in just five of them.

THE PLAYBOOK THEY ALL SHARE

Eight patterns that repeat across every firm above that is actually working.

  1. The hero headline names the customer, not the service. "for contractors," "for Real Estate Investors," "FOR TRUCK DRIVERS," "For Roofers." None of them lead with credentials, location or years in business. The one firm here that softened to a generic hero has the worst traffic-per-keyword ratio on the list.
  2. Money pages beat blog posts, badly. Profitability Partners gets 1,191 visits from 93 posts. bookskeep gets 103 from 391. The difference is pages that answer the exact operator question versus general accounting articles.
  3. Own the named concepts the niche already says out loud. Retainage, WIP schedules, percentage of completion, job costing, Section 179 on service trucks. No generalist CPA competes for these, and the umbrella table above shows they carry real volume.
  4. Publish the price, or at least publish a number. Level's ~3% of gross. Contractor In Charge's $400/week line items. Passageway's $650/$1,000/$1,300. The Roofers CFO's $2,000 Challenge. Public numbers pre-qualify leads who will never meet you in person, which is the whole problem with going national.
  5. Give the industry a tool it cannot get anywhere else. Exit value calculators, per-trade job costing calculators, subcontractor packets, owner-operator tools. These earn links, rank for operational searches, and prove fluency in about four seconds.
  6. Borrow an audience before building one. Every firm here that grew fast attached to an existing gathering point: a podcast circuit, a book, a conference, a trades YouTuber. None waited for SEO to compound on its own.
  7. Productize into a named ladder. SmartStart to SmartBooks to SmartCFO. Operational to Tactical to Strategic. It lets a $1M contractor start small and makes the CFO upsell feel like a graduation rather than a new sale.
  8. Authority Score tracks niche discipline, not firm size. The range across these eleven firms is 2 to 35, and it lines up almost exactly with how long and how narrowly each stayed on one vertical. A realistic 12-month target is the 14 to 21 band, which on this evidence means roughly 100 to 150 well-targeted pages plus one borrowed-audience channel.
