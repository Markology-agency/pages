Income From Six Places and Withholding From None

It usually starts small. Payouts land in your personal checking account. The camera, the lighting, the trip you filmed, and the editing software all go on a personal card.

Then tax season shows up. A 1099 arrives from a platform you'd forgotten about, and you're sorting a year of bank statements trying to remember which charges were for the channel.

The bigger surprise is the bill. When nobody withholds for you, the tax on a good year all comes due at once, plus self-employment tax on top of income tax.

The fix starts with one account. Then a plan.

what we do

What We Handle for Content Creators

01

One business account

An LLC and a dedicated business bank account. Every payout goes in and every business expense goes out. The statement becomes your record, so you don't have to save a receipt for every small purchase. Receipts matter for the bigger ones that could go either way, like an order that might be a studio light or might be a TV.

02

The deduction test

If you wouldn't have spent the money except for the business, it's likely deductible. For creators that's often equipment, software, props, studio space, and travel with a real business purpose. Personal things you now use for the business, like your phone and laptop, count for the business share. I'll tell you where the line is.

03

Quarterly payments you can actually make

Creator income is lumpy. A big brand deal can land in one month and nothing the next. We set a percentage of what comes in, you move it to savings, and the estimated payments come out of that account.

04

The S-corp, when it pays

An S-corp can lower self-employment tax, but it adds payroll, a separate return, and a higher prep fee. I only recommend it once the income is steady and big enough that the savings clear those costs by a wide margin. Not before.

Family on the Payroll, Done Properly

A lot of creator businesses are family businesses in practice. I have clients with a photography business on the side who bring their kids along to shoots. The kids do real work, they're paid a fair wage for it, and that wage is a business deduction.

How it's set up matters. The payroll tax rules for children employed by a parent are different for a sole proprietorship than for an S-corp. We'll pick the structure with that in mind and keep a simple record of the work.

questions

Questions Content Creators Ask

Do I need an LLC for my creator income?+

You don't need one to be taxed as a business. A single-member LLC is taxed the same as if you earned the money personally, reported on Schedule C. I still recommend one, along with a dedicated bank account, because it keeps the business activity separate and makes a later S-corp election cleaner.

Which expenses are deductible for a content creator?+

Expenses you take on to earn the income. That usually means equipment, software, props, studio space, and travel with a real business purpose. A dedicated room at home can qualify for the home office deduction. Clothes you'd wear anyway usually don't count.

How do I pay quarterly taxes on irregular income?+

Move a set percentage of every payout into a separate savings account as it arrives. Each quarter, send the IRS what the plan says. In December we build a projection and true up the last payment.

When does an S-corp make sense?+

Once the income is consistent and big enough that the payroll tax savings clearly beat the added cost of payroll and a separate return. We run the numbers before anyone files Form 2553.
