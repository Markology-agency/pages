The honest read on the second half

The firm has won Cape Cod search. That is a genuine achievement and it happened faster than the January plan assumed. The difficulty is that the terms now held at position 1 carry very little monthly search volume, mostly between zero and ten searches a month. Ranking first on a term nobody searches produces very few enquiries.

This means more SEO effort against the same Cape Cod terms will not move enquiries much, because there is no more demand there to capture. Growth in the second half has to come from reaching more people rather than ranking higher for the same ones. Three routes are open, in the order we would recommend.

01

Build real pages for Plymouth, Wareham, Bourne, Carver and Marion

Advertising already targets these towns, but every click currently lands on a page written about Cape Cod. The South Shore and South Coast have several times the population of the Cape's year-round base, and the firm has no dedicated page for any of it.

Why this first: it is the cheapest way to add search demand rather than compete harder for the demand already captured, and it improves the advertising results at the same time.

02

Turn on Local Services Ads and start measuring phone calls

Local Services Ads place a Google-screened listing above the paid results and charge per enquiry rather than per click. For a local CPA this is the closest fit of any channel available. Alongside it, call tracking would finally show how many enquiries the phone produces, which is currently invisible.

Why this second: it adds a new source of enquiries without depending on search rankings, and it closes the measurement gap that makes the 7.5 figure unreliable.

03

Start the personalized follow-up that the January plan called for

The plan set out a dedicated path for each type of client, each with its own lead magnet, webinar topic and follow-up. None of it was built. The practical starting point is not the full structure but follow-up sequences for the enquiries the firm already receives, so that a quiet month like June does not simply pass.

Why this third: it raises the value of every enquiry already being generated, which is cheaper than generating more, and it is the largest piece of the January plan still outstanding.

Also worth testing in the second half

  • A live talk to a Cape chamber or business group, recorded once and reused as the quarterly webinar. This satisfies the webinar commitment and produces referrals in a room, which suits a local firm better than an online-only event.
  • Paid social aimed at seasonal operators, most usefully in the winter when tax preparation demand is at its lowest and cash-flow worries are at their highest.
  • A small advertising test on ChatGPT, which opened to businesses of any size in May 2026 and now targets by postcode. Worth a few hundred dollars a month to learn from, not a channel to commit to yet.
  • Recovering the healthcare accounting page, which fell from position 1 to 31 and represents traffic the firm previously held.

Three technical items still outstanding

These sit inside the website administration area and need to be actioned there. Each one is currently holding back work that has already been completed.

  • Clear the sitemap cache. Four main service pages were rewritten on August 4, but the file that tells Google when pages changed still reports December 2025. Until it is cleared, Google has no reason to look at the new content.
  • Redirect the old Atlantic Payroll address. The address /what-we-do/atlantic-payroll still receives more organic visits than almost any other page and currently sends every one of them to the home page instead of the payroll page.
  • Remove the crawl delay instruction from the site's robots file, which currently asks search engines to wait ten seconds between pages.
