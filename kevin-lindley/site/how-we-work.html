How Working With Us Works, From the First Call to the Filed Return

Most people who reach out to Kestrel have already had a CPA. What they haven’t had is someone who told them what to expect, laid out a plan for the year and then checked in before the year was over.

This page walks through exactly how that works with us. It starts with the first conversation and covers your first 30 days, then the year that follows. You’ll see your tax road map before your first payment is due, and if it isn’t worth it to you, you can cancel at that point.

before you hire us

Before You Hire Us

You don’t need to have your questions figured out before we talk. Most people start with a short list and a general feeling that they’re paying more than they should. There are three steps before any work begins.

1
Tell me what’s going on

Pick a time on the calendar. If you want to send a few details first, like how you earn and what's changing this year, email them over. I read them before the call, so we don't spend half of it on the basics.

2
The intro call

It’s a 30-minute video call, and there’s no cost or obligation. We start with your situation and I tell you what I’d look at first. If you don’t need me yet, I’ll tell you that too. Some people are better off with TurboTax for another year, and I’d rather say so than take your money.

3
A written proposal

After the call, you upload last year’s return so I can look it over for anything that carries into this year. Then I send a fee quote and an engagement letter. It spells out what we’ll handle, what stays with you or another advisor, the fee and what your first 30 days look like. It’s wordy on purpose, and if anything in it is unclear, we can hop on another call before you sign. Nothing starts until you’ve read it and signed it.

Once it’s signed, the real work starts.

Your First 30 Days

The first month is when we learn how your money actually moves. By the end of it, you’ll have a plan you can look at and question before you’ve paid anything for the year.

DAY 0
Sign and schedule

You sign the engagement letter, put a payment method on file and book your onboarding call.

WEEKS 1 TO 4
Review and build

We go through your prior returns, your entity, the books, payroll and the tax payments you’ve already made. Anything that’s off gets flagged, and we build your first tax road map. If last year’s return still needs to be filed, we wrap that up first, so this year’s plan starts from the right numbers.

DAY 30

We go over the road map on a call. You see what we found, what we’d do first and what we need from you. Your first payment isn’t charged until this point.

See your road map before the first payment. If it isn’t worth it to you, cancel with no notice period and no cancellation fee.

If you’re starting a new job or a new contract in a couple of months, we can set the start date around it so you have some breathing room. After that first month, onboarding is mostly paperwork, and we keep it short.

onboarding

What Onboarding Looks Like

Some firms start by asking for every document you’ve ever touched. We only ask for what we need to understand how things are set up today.

Welcome and access

You get a welcome email and a secure portal of your own. That’s your home base, where documents get uploaded, invoices get paid and anything that needs your signature gets signed. Please don’t email your W-2. The portal is there so you don’t have to.

What we ask for first

We start with last year’s personal and business returns, your entity formation papers and your S corporation election if you have one. We also ask for your current books, payroll reports, the estimated payments you’ve made and details on any retirement plan you contribute to.

Two short questionnaires

The first one covers business costs you paid out of a personal account, because almost everyone has some in the first year. The second one asks about vehicle use, a home office, equipment, reimbursements and family members who work in the business. That tells us which deductions actually fit your situation and what records we’ll need to back them up.

From there, the work follows the calendar.

over the year

What Happens Over the Year

The road map you see in your first month turns into a plan that follows the year. Each quarter has a job, and the December projection feeds next year’s return.

Q1File and align

We file last year’s returns and check them against last year’s plan. If the return and the projection don’t line up, we figure out why.

Q2Map the year

We update the tax road map, a month-by-month view of your income from every source and what goes to tax. It shows what to set aside, what each estimated payment should be and whether an entity change is worth a closer look.

Q3Adjust

New contracts, new hires and big purchases go into the plan as they come up. Summer is also when we revise the road map with real numbers from the first half of the year.

Q4Project

In December, eleven months have already happened. We build a projection from real numbers and work through the what-ifs while there’s still time to act on them. That’s also when we set the final estimated payment.

Filing on extension moves the filing date, and this year’s planning carries on as normal. By the time we file, we’re mostly carrying out decisions we already made together.
between meetings

Can I Ask Questions Between Meetings?

Yes, and I’d rather you did. Every engagement letter includes a line called “a reasonable amount of consultation.” It’s vague on purpose, because the questions that matter don’t arrive on a schedule.

Say you’re offered a new contract in August, or you’re thinking about buying a car through the business. You might be wondering how much to set aside for taxes after a big month. Those are the questions to send while the answer can still change something.

New contractHiring someoneBuying equipment or propertyChanging your salaryHow much to set asideA job offer to compare

Call, text or email, and you’ll hear back within a day or two, even if the answer is that I’m looking into it. You won’t get a bill for asking.

your other advisors

Working With Your Other Advisors

Most of our clients already have a few professionals in their corner. The trouble starts when each of them is working from a different set of numbers and you end up carrying information between them.

We work with the people you already have, or take on more of the work ourselves if you’d prefer that.

Financial advisor

We share the tax picture so contribution and investment decisions line up with the plan.

Retirement plan administrator

We work out how much can go in, from whom and by when.

Attorney

We sort out the tax side of entity formation and ownership changes before the documents are drafted.

Banker or lender

We provide financial statements a lender can actually use.

Payroll provider

We set owner salary and withholding so payroll matches the plan.

Bookkeeper

We agree on how the books are kept, so the numbers we plan from are current.

We don’t give investment advice, and we don’t act as your retirement plan administrator. We make sure the tax side of those decisions is understood before you make them.

less to manage

You Shouldn’t Have to Manage Your CPA

If you’ve ever had to remind your accountant about a deadline, you know how this usually goes. You end up chasing documents, remembering due dates, checking that payroll ran correctly, tracking retirement contributions and following up with everyone involved.

With us, that tracking is our job. When something needs a decision, we bring you the decision, the numbers behind it, our recommendation and the deadline. We also tell you exactly what we need from you to get it done.

The decisions are still yours. I’ll tell you where the line is on a deduction and what the risk looks like, and you choose how close you want to stand to it.
