the problem

You Hear About the Strategies. Nobody Shows You How to Run Them.

You paid a lot in tax last year and it stings. You've read about the home office, paying your kids, the S-corp, renting your house to your business. You bring one of them up and your accountant either says it's too aggressive or doesn't say anything at all.

That's the thing I hear most on intro calls. People know the strategies exist. What they don't have is somebody to say which ones apply to them, in what order, and then go do it.

I'll be honest about the other side of it too. There is no one thing that makes the taxes go away, and I'm not going to pretend there's a magic wand. Taxes are the single largest cash expense most families have. What works is a lot of little things that add up to a much better result, and almost all of them have to happen before December 31.

That's what proactive tax planning is. It has three parts.

The Tax Road Map, the December Projection, and the Line

The tax road map

Early in the year I take last year's return and this year's expected income from every source, yours and your spouse's if you file together, and map it month by month. It's honestly just a spreadsheet, but it tells you what goes to tax, what goes to retirement, what your deductions are worth, and what each quarterly payment should be. It's the difference between guessing and having a number.

The December projection

By December, January through November has already happened. We pull the real numbers, guess on one month instead of twelve, and build a draft of your tax return. Then we play the what-if game. What if you max fund the retirement plan? What if you buy the vehicle this year instead of next? Do we run a bonus payroll? There are still four weeks left to act, and that's the whole point.

Where the line is

Every deduction comes with the risk explained. The best predictor I've found for how aggressive someone wants to be on taxes is how they invest. If you're in cash and bonds, we'll stay conservative. If you're leveraged into crypto, you'll want every legitimate lever pulled. I'll tell you where the line is, and how close you stand to it is your call. I'll also tell you plainly when something crosses it.

how it works

How Tax Planning Works Through the Year

Q1: File and align

Last year's returns, checked against last year's plan. If the projection and the return don't line up, we figure out why.

Q2: Map the year

The tax road map. Income, set-asides, deductions, and the entity decision if there is one.

Q3: Adjust

A new contract, a new hire, a purchase, a move, a job offer to compare. Bring the question and we update the plan. That's what the consultation is for.

Q4: Project

The December projection and the final estimated payment, with time left to act on what it shows.

questions

Tax Planning Questions We Get Asked

What's the difference between tax preparation and tax planning?+

Tax preparation is typing in the boxes and filing what already happened. Tax planning is looking at the year while it's still happening, because most of the moves that lower a bill have to be made before December 31, not in April.

When should I elect S-corporation status?+

Usually once self-employment income is your main income and there's enough of it that running payroll pays for itself. If most of your income is still W-2, the election does less than you'd expect, because you've already paid in most of your Social Security through that W-2. We run the math before anyone files Form 2553, and the decision usually doesn't have to be made until late in the year.

How do quarterly estimated tax payments work?+

Once you're self-employed, nobody is withholding for you, so the IRS expects estimated payments through the year. I keep it simple: take what came in last quarter, apply the percentage we set in your road map, send it in. Then the December projection trues up the last payment, sometimes to almost nothing.

What percentage should I set aside for tax?+

It depends on your deductions, how much you're putting into retirement, and whether there's a state involved, so I won't give you one number for everybody. What I will do is give you your number in the road map, and we set it a little high for the first three quarters so the fourth-quarter conversation is about what to do with the extra, not how to cover a shortfall.
