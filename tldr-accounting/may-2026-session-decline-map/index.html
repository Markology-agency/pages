Bucket A · The Big One

THE SALARY PAGE IS
35% OF YOUR TRAFFIC.

One URL: /starting-salary-for-therapists-by-state-2024/. That page ranks for more than 40 salary keyword variants. "Therapist salary" alone is 12,100 searches a month. The slug has "2024" in it, the position dropped from earlier 2026 levels, and Google demotes year-stamped pages once newer competitor content appears.

The diagnosis

Three signals point at the same cause:

  • Slug staleness. The URL ends in "2024." Google increasingly penalizes year-stamped slugs when newer content from competitors (Indeed, ZipRecruiter, Healthline) refreshes with 2025-2026 figures. Searchers also visually skip dated results.
  • Position regression. "Therapist salary" sits at position 12 on a 12,100/mo keyword. The keyword has been flat all year (Trends 0.81 to 1.00). The position drop, not the search demand, is what changed.
  • Trickle effect. About 25 long-tail variants ("average therapy salary," "how much do therapists make a year," "how much money does a therapist make") all live on this same URL. When the main page drops, the whole cluster drops.
The fix
  • Add 300 to 400 words updating salary data to 2025 Bureau of Labor Statistics figures (BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook is the canonical source, published annually).
  • Update H1, page title, and meta description to remove "2024" or reference "current" data.
  • Push the published/modified date to today on the next CMS save.
  • Add internal links from 3 to 5 newer blog posts pointing to this page with descriptive anchor text.
  • Do not change the URL slug. A 301 redirect carries link-equity risk on a page this heavy. The freshness signals above will move the needle without the redirect.
  • After publish, ping GSC URL Inspection > Request Indexing on the URL.
Expected recovery: If the page moves from position 12 back to position 7 on "therapist salary" (where it sat earlier this year), the math conservatively returns 300 to 500 monthly visits. That alone closes most of the 700-session gap from peak.
Bucket B · Don't Worry

SEASONAL PAGES.
NO ACTION NEEDED.

Two URLs lost impressions for one reason: April 15 ended. They will rebound next January.

The two seasonal URLs
  • /what-does-it-mean-when-you-sign-your-8879/. "8879" search volume peaks in February and March (tax filing). The page held position 4 the entire time. Impressions follow the calendar, not the SEO.
  • /restricted-stock-unit-taxes-and-your-w-2/. RSU / W-2 box 14 queries are tax-season-only. The Trends data shows volume crashes from 0.79 in March to 0.01 by April. The page never moved positions; the searchers just went home.
Why this matters for the meeting

Roughly half of the Feb-to-April decline is these two pages losing tax-season search volume. That portion of the dip is not a problem to solve. It's a calendar. Removing it from concern frees attention for the bucket-A salary page fix and the Seattle expansion.

Bucket C · The EFTPS Question

EFTPS LOGIN BRINGS
TRAFFIC. NOT LEADS.

The /federal-tax-payment-system/ page ranks position 28 for "eftps login" (60,500 searches a month). The traffic share is real but the keyword is a brand login query, not a therapist commercial intent. People searching "eftps login" want the IRS login page, not a CPA.

The decision

This is the page-level evidence behind the defend-vs-decay question in the strategy memo. EFTPS, IRS payment plan, and similar legacy queries bring impressions but almost no leads. Two paths:

  • Defend. Refresh the page, add a current-year IRS payment guidance section, push internal links. Cost: writing time that would otherwise go to therapist niche.
  • Let decay. Accept that this is a non-converting traffic source and reinvest the time into Seattle expansion and therapist niche.
Markology recommendation: Decay. The therapist-niche pages (Bucket D) convert. EFTPS does not. Keeping the page live but not actively investing time in it preserves whatever residual value it has without trading niche capacity.
