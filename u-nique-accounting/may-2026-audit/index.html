What This Means for Recovery

This is the type of decline that core updates produce when content lacks demonstrable expertise signals (author identity, original data, recency, depth). The fact that two consecutive core updates hit means Google is consistently scoring this content lower than competitors.

Recovery levers in order of impact

  • E-E-A-T sprint on the top 8 losing pages. Author bylines with credentials, real client examples, original data points, last-updated dates, link to the U-Nique team page. These are the highest-impression pages currently still indexed. Recovering even 30% of Sept levels on these 8 pages restores roughly 4,000 weekly impressions.
  • Investigate the brand-query loss. Search "unique accounting" and "u-nique accounting" in incognito and document who is outranking the homepage. If the SERP shows a directory listing, GBP, or a third-party review site above u-niqueaccounting.com, that's a separate fix (homepage SEO refresh + branded backlink plan).
  • Refresh /p-l-for-restaurants/ and /s-corp-vs-llc-for-restaurants/. Both fell from page 1–2 to position 100+. These are recoverable with a content depth + keyword refresh, but they need to climb back into the top 100 first to start collecting impressions again.
  • Retire bottom-decile content. Pages still indexed but never receiving impressions are net-negative for site quality scoring. Identify and either consolidate or 301 to a stronger sibling page.

What is NOT the problem

  • No manual action (sample URL inspection clean across the board).
  • No robots.txt or noindex blocks.
  • No site migration or URL structure change between October 2025 and now.
  • No canonical conflicts (Google's chosen canonical matches the declared canonical on every spot-checked URL).
  • No sitemap submission errors or warnings.
