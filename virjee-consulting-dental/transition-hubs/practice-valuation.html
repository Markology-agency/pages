What EBITDA can hide

EBITDA is a good starting point. Omar calls it the first dirty version of the X-ray. Before you trust it, you need a cleaner image.

Owner pay that's well under market makes profit look bigger than it is. A spouse working as office manager for a token wage does the same thing. One-off spending, like a big year of continuing education, can push it the other way. We adjust for all of it.

Who orders a written valuation

Owners selling in the next couple of years, so they go into the sale knowing their number.

Owners five or ten years out, so they know which levers to work on.

Partners planning a buy-in or buy-out, and owners going through a divorce or estate plan who need a number on paper.

Questions we hear a lot

How much is a dental practice worth?

It depends mostly on true profit, once the owner's pay is set to market. Location, the lease, equipment age and how much depends on the owner all move it up or down. The calculator gives you a range; a written valuation gives you a number you can use.

Is a calculator good enough?

For a first look, yes. For a sale, a partner buy-in or a bank, you'll want the written version.
