Core Advisors · Google Ads landing page

Why the ad clicks aren't turning into calls

Most of Core's Google Ads budget goes to one page, coreadvisors.com/dental-financial-team/. We watched it in Hotjar for the last 30 days and compared that with the ad data. These wireframes show the changes we'd make to that page.

What's happening on the page today

The page has eleven sections, and the only form sits near the bottom, about 83% of the way down. Every "Book a call" button jumps down to it. The form asks seven questions and then sends people to a separate calendar to pick a time, so a dentist has to say yes twice before anything is booked.

1Take the full menu off the ad page

Keep the logo and one "Book a call" button. Move the menu links into the footer.

The only clicks Hotjar recorded were on the menu ("Our Team" and "Your Practice"). Each one took a paid visitor off the page.

2Put a short form in the hero

Name, email and one tap to say whether they own a practice. The form sits next to the headline, so nobody has to scroll to find it.

Only a third of desktop visitors scrolled far enough to see today's form, and nobody clicked a "Book a call" button.

2Use the owner question to screen out junk

Owners go straight to Jeremiah's calendar. Associates and non-dentists get a thank-you page with the Sellability Check and the newsletter, so they don't book a sales call.

The form leads from these ads weren't real prospects. The revenue question can move to the calendar step or the call itself.

3Say what happens on the call

Three short steps, using the promise already on the page: "If we're a fit, we'll tell you what the first 90 days look like."

Right now that line sits at the very bottom, next to a form most visitors never reach.

4Move the team and reviews up

Faces and Google reviews come right after the call steps, with "Meet everyone" opening the team on the same page.

"Our Team" was the most clicked link. People want to see who they'll be talking to.

5Keep one story section

The timeline makes the point well. Cut "Why we exist" and "Why Core", which say the same thing again, and add a button under the timeline.

Fewer sections between the hero and the next button means more visitors see it.

6Services open in place

Short cards that expand instead of linking away.

"Your Practice" was the second most clicked link, so visitors want the service detail. Give it to them without sending them away.

7Book on the same page

Show Jeremiah's calendar inline after the short form, instead of redirecting to Calendly.

Today a dentist fills in seven fields, then has to start again on a different site to pick a time.

8Add a sticky bottom bar on phones

One "Book a call" button stays at the bottom of the screen the whole way down and opens the short form.

Half of the paid clicks come from phones, and on a phone today's form is a long scroll away.

9Two fields and one tap above the fold

Name and email fields with large tap targets, and the owner question as buttons instead of radio circles.

Hotjar only recorded 2 phone sessions, so we'll check this again once the new page has traffic.

Outside the page

Some of the clicks weren't from people looking for a dental CPA. The most expensive search term since August was "denticon", which is dental practice software. Searches for competitors by name, like Dominic Lobifaro, also cost money. We'll block "denticon" and the remaining competitor names so the budget goes to searches like "dental cpa".

Once the new page is live, we'll judge it on booked calls from practice owners, not form fills.
