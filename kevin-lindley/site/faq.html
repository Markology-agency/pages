Tax Planning and Tax Preparation

What's the difference between tax preparation and tax planning?
−

Preparation is compliance: filling in the numbers, dropping them into the software, and filing on time. Planning is figuring out what the IRS actually permits you to deduct and doing it while the year is still open. The return should confirm the plan, not introduce you to a different version of your year. See tax planning for business owners.

How do quarterly estimated tax payments work?
−

Once nobody is withholding for you, the IRS expects estimated tax payments through the year. We map out where those payments need to land in your tax road map, so you're not figuring it out haphazardly each quarter. The last payment comes from the December projection, when we're only guessing on one month instead of twelve.

What's the difference between a 1099 and a W-2?
−

With a W-2, your employer withholds your taxes, pays half of your payroll tax, and usually covers health insurance and a 401(k) match. With a 1099, you're both the employee and the employer, so you pay the full self-employment tax yourself. That means the same dollar figure is not the same pay. The biggest upside of a 1099 is how much more you can put into a tax-deferred retirement plan. See CPA for the self-employed.

S-Corps and Entities

When should I elect S-corporation status?
−

When the tax benefit clearly covers the added cost, and not before. S-corps are great, but they come with payroll, a full set of books, and a separate return. If you already have a large W-2, your employer is covering most of your payroll tax, so the S-corp does a lot less. It also rarely makes sense for a short stint of 1099 work. We run the math before anyone files Form 2553.

What counts as a reasonable salary for an S-corp owner?
−

The IRS requires S-corp owners who work in the business to pay themselves reasonable compensation for their services. There's a gray area in how far you push it. In a very specialized field, most of the income may need to run through as salary. Where salaries vary widely, there's more room. We set it against the payroll tax savings and the retirement funding you want.

Do I need a CPA if I already have a bookkeeper?
−

A bookkeeper records what happened. A CPA plans around it and files. If you want to do the books and you're good at it, you should do the books. If you never want to touch them, we'll take them over. Either way, the books have to feed the tax plan. See bookkeeping in Plano.

Texas Taxes

Does Texas have a state income tax?
−

No. Texas has no state personal income tax. The state collects through franchise tax, sales tax, property tax, and others instead. State income tax only comes back into the picture when you earn in another state.

What is the Texas franchise tax and do I have to file it?
−

It's an annual tax on most entities formed or doing business in Texas, and the annual report is due May 15. If your entity is at or below the no-tax-due threshold, you still file a Public Information Report or Ownership Report, but you owe no tax. We keep the filing on the calendar so it doesn't lapse.

Working With Us

How much does a CPA cost for a small business?
−

It depends on the entity, the books, and how many owner-level deductions we're running. We bill a flat monthly retainer, quoted after the intro call once I've seen your prior return. A reasonable amount of consultation is part of the fee. If all you need is a filing, I'll tell you that too. If you're a straightforward W-2, I'll tell you TurboTax is probably fine.

What should a physician look for in a CPA?
−

Someone who already knows what a locums contract, a PLLC, a reasonable salary for your specialty, and a practice startup look like. You shouldn't have to teach your accountant how you get paid. And they should answer when you call. See CPA for physicians.

Can you work with me if I earn in more than one state?
−

Yes. I have clients all over the country, and most of them meet with me over video. The technically correct answer on another state is usually that you need to file there. Then the real question is materiality: how much you earned there, the state's rules, and the risk. I'll lay that out and you decide.

Who will I actually be working with?

Me. A handful of people help part-time with bookkeeping and basic prep, but they won't really be emailing you. You won't be onboarded by one person and passed off to someone else.
