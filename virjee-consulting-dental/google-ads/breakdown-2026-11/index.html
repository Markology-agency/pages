Virjee Consulting

Google Ads keyword and campaign breakdown

Both ad accounts: the dental account for dentalcpausa.com and the medical account for virjeecpa.com. Dental covers the last 90 days (Aug 8 to Nov 6, 2026) and October on its own. Medical covers Oct 1 to Nov 6.

What's really going on

The causes first, with how sure we are. The numbers behind each one are further down.

Four dental keywords bring in every non-brand form lead

Over 90 days the dental search campaign spent $1,713 and produced 6 form leads. All 6 came from four keywords that spent $882 between them. The other 31 keywords that got clicks spent $832 and produced none.

The best of the four, "dental cpa" (phrase match), brought in 3 of the 6 at about $196 each. It was paused during October, and October's non-brand search produced 1 form lead against 3 in September on similar spend.

Confidence: medium. The keyword split is certain. Whether the pause caused the October dip is likely but not proven, because at 1 to 3 leads a month a single month can swing on its own.

The dental Performance Max campaign brings cheap clicks but not owners

Since it started on Oct 11 it spent $402 for 301 clicks, about $1.34 each, and produced 2 form leads plus one call over a minute. Both form leads were people who don't own a practice yet: a 1099 associate and someone starting a practice.

Confidence: medium. The two named leads are matched by their ad click IDs. Two leads is too few to judge the campaign for good.

The "medical" campaign mostly reaches people searching for a Houston CPA

The largest search group we can see for Performance Max - Medical is "certified public accountant houston tx", followed by "best cpa near me" and "tax consultants near me". From Oct 1 to Nov 6 it produced 3 form leads and 10 calls over a minute. None of the form leads owned a practice: one 1099 medical practitioner and one who chose "Other".

Calls were 10 of its 13 leads, and we turned call conversions off on Oct 30 because you want form leads. Since then the campaign has drifted to YouTube: from Oct 31 to Nov 6, 128 of its 136 clicks came from YouTube and only 4 came from Google search.

Confidence: high on where the clicks went. Medium on the reason. Losing its main conversion signal is the usual cause of that kind of shift, and the timing matches exactly.

The medical search campaign has not produced a lead since Oct 1

Search Campaign - Medical Accounting Firm spent $209 on 38 clicks with no forms and no calls. Google only shows us the searches behind $19 of that spend, so negative keywords can't fix it. The searches we can see include competitor names, nurses and hospital accounting.

Confidence: high. This is a straight read of the account.

virjeecpa.com's practice owners come from organic search, not from the ads

From Oct 1 to Nov 6, virjeecpa.com received 23 form leads. Seven came from people who own a practice, and five of those found the site through organic search. None came from an ad.

Confidence: high for leads since Oct 2, when the form started recording the ad click ID.

Ranked improvements

Ordered by how much each should help form leads from practice owners. No changes have been made yet.

  1. Pause the medical search campaign and move its $4 a day to dental non-brand search.
    Evidence: $209 and 38 clicks since Oct 1 with no form leads and no calls. 91% of its spend went to searches Google doesn't show, so we can't filter them out. Dental non-brand search produced the only practice-owner form lead from a non-brand ad this period (Rupesh Pandey, Nov 2, keyword "dental practice cpa").
  2. Turn "dental cpa" (phrase match) back on in the dental search campaign.
    Evidence: 3 of the 6 non-brand form leads in 90 days came from this keyword, at about $196 each against $286 for the campaign overall. The exact-match version that stayed on spent $132 with no form leads, so the phrase version is reaching the searches that convert, such as "dental cpa near me" and "dental cpa california".
  3. Give Performance Max - Medical a 30-day form-only test with these negative keywords, then decide.
    Evidence: these searches are for tax prep, tax offices, jobs and lawyers, none of which describes a practice owner. With calls off, judge it only on form leads. If it brings fewer than 2 form leads and no practice owners by Dec 6, cut it to $5 a day and move the rest to dental non-brand search.

    Evidence: 2 form leads so far, neither an owner. Its clicks are cheap, so it costs little to keep learning, but it isn't yet earning more budget.

    Evidence: these got clicks with no leads and point to a competitor, to brokers, or to people reading a checklist. The amounts are small, so this is tidying up.
