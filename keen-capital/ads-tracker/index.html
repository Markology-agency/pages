RIA CPL Framing

WHAT $50 PER LEAD
ACTUALLY MEANS.

RIA lead-gen runs on different math than accounting or restaurant CFO accounts. The sales cycle is longer, the qualified-prospect bar is higher, and the lifetime value of a single HNW client justifies a CPL multiple of what an SMB practice would tolerate. Three benchmarks to keep in view before treating the current $50.71 cost-per-conv as either "too high" or "too low."

Typical RIA HNW CPL range
$75 - $200
Industry benchmark for HNW prospect lead-gen via paid search. Below this band is suspiciously cheap (worth checking the tracked event definition). Above this band is normal until late funnel converts.
Current Keen Capital CPL (30d)
$50.71
Below the typical RIA band. Either a strong early signal OR a sign that the tracked conversion event is a soft action (page view, scroll) rather than a real inquiry. Tracking audit is what tells the difference.
Sales cycle, HNW prospect to client
60 - 180 days
RIA HNW prospects rarely convert on a single touch. A "conversion" in Google Ads is the start of a sales journey, not the end. Optimizing on a 30-day window means optimizing for cycle-start signals, not closes.
The framing: Don't treat the $50.71 CPL like a Vast CFO or BizBud-type accounting lead. RIA economics support spending more per lead because each closed HNW client represents a multi-year AUM relationship. The question isn't "is $50 cheap?" — it's "is the event that triggered the $50 count a real qualified prospect signal?" That's what the conversion-tracking audit answers.
Open Decision

WHAT COUNTS AS
A CONVERSION?

The blocker on every ad reactivation: defining the conversion event Thomas considers a "qualified lead." Four candidate events live on the site today. Each one optimizes the campaign toward a different downstream behavior.

Conversion Event Definition · Open

Which behavior is the Google Ads optimization target?

Until this is locked, the audit task can't fully close and the A/B campaigns can't relaunch with confidence. Markology recommends choosing one primary event and demoting the others to secondary signals.

Calendly bookingHighest-intent event. A booked discovery call is a real qualified-prospect signal. Lowest volume of the four, so optimization signal is sparse on the current $200/month spend. Best primary event when budget scales up.
Contact form submissionMid-intent. Some real prospects, some general inquiries. Higher volume than Calendly so the optimization algorithm has more to learn from. Common default for HNW lead-gen accounts. Markology recommendation for current spend scale.
Phone call (call tracking)High-intent but underused on the current site. Requires call-tracking number setup. Worth adding as a secondary conversion once budget scales.
Financial-snapshot intakeHighest-intent of all if the intake form goes deep (assets, goals, timeline). Lowest-volume. Best as a bottom-funnel event for retargeting and downstream nurture, not as the primary ads optimization signal.

Markology recommendation: Contact form as primary conversion event. Calendly booking as secondary. Both reported in Google Ads, with Smart Bidding optimizing on contact form. Re-evaluate the split once monthly spend crosses $1,000 and Calendly bookings have enough volume to optimize against directly.
