Conversion tracking needs fixing

  • Why it reads zero: the setup itself is right. Contact Form Submission (7641145177, SUBMIT_LEAD_FORM, primary) is the only action and the only biddable goal at account and campaign level. Its label AW-18225010125/1OpUCNm-yrscEM2rrvJD matches the live site code on /, /contact-us, /internal-audit-co-sourcing and /internal-audit-quality-assessment, and both ad landing pages carry the tracked form (#wf-form-Contact-Us-Form). So the most likely reason for zero is that only 97 people clicked in five months, at about $10 a click, which is not enough traffic to expect a lead yet.
  • The site code only counts the form if the Webflow success box (.w-form-done) appears within 6 seconds of submit (20 checks, 300ms apart). The form uses Cloudflare Turnstile, so a slow check could make a real lead go uncounted. A test submission from an ad-style URL (with ?gclid=test) would settle it.
  • RFP leads count through a page-load tag on /rfp-thank-you (same label). The RFP form is a monday.com iframe, so it only counts if that form sends the visitor to /rfp-thank-you. Nobody has confirmed that end to end.
  • Nobody has compared the Webflow form submissions (which carry utm and referrer since 2026-09-21) against ad clicks. If a submission came from a Google Ads click and Google shows zero, tracking is broken. If none did, zero is real.
  • The client scorecard still lists 'Google Ads conversion import' as not connected, so there is no CRM or offline signal to back up the tag.

What we target

All of the United States, set to 'presence or interest'. 37 enabled keywords, all phrase or exact. Search network only, with no search partners and no display. 164 campaign negatives. No audiences. Manual CPC.

  • Location is set to presence or interest. $310.30 of the $982.67 (34 of 97 clicks) was matched by 'area of interest' rather than people physically in the US. Switching to presence only fits a US firm.
  • Budget is far too small for the click price. At about $10 a click, $6/day buys less than one click a day, and the campaign lost 66% of its possible impressions to budget and 22% to rank (13% impression share). At this pace it would take years to reach the 10-lead proof bar.
  • Only $105 of the $730 spent Aug 1 to Oct 30 shows a visible search term, so about 86% of the spend can't be checked for junk.
  • Quality Score is 1 to 3 on most of the spending keywords ('internal audit advisory' QS 2, 'internal audit company' QS 1, 'internal audit quality assessment' QS 3), which means Google rates the ad and page fit as weak and charges more per click.
  • The broad negative 'intern' from the September incident is still in place. It doesn't block 'internal' searches, because negatives don't match partial words, but it can go now that the routine is fixed.
