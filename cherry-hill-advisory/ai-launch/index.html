SUBJECT: Four Topical Requirements are issued. Most plans still handle one. Here is the thing nobody wants to say in a planning meeting: treating the IIA's Topical Requirements as a queue is going to age badly. The queue made sense when Cybersecurity was the only one with a date on it. It isn't anymore. The IIA released Third-Party with an effective date of September 15, 2026. Organizational Behavior follows on December 15, 2026. Organizational Resilience on April 30, 2027. Two more are moving through consultation behind them. So the plan that says "we'll do Cybersecurity this year and pick up Third-Party next year" is not a plan. It is a queue with four things in it and eighteen months of runway, and the requirements do not wait their turn. The part that gets missed: these overlap. Third-party access reviews are evidence for Cybersecurity. Escalation behavior shows up in both Organizational Behavior and Resilience. A function working the list sequentially tests the same control three times and reports it three ways. A function working it as one map tests it once. Whether your conformance is assessed one requirement at a time is not up to you. At your next external quality assessment it is assessed against what is in effect on that date, in whatever order you got there. Full stop. SO TEST IT, RIGHT NOW. We built a free screener that tells you where your function stands against each issued Topical Requirement. No call, no proposal, no salesperson. Answer the questions, see the gaps. Subject: Four Topical Requirements are issued. Most plans still handle one.
Here is the thing nobody wants to say in a planning meeting: treating the IIA's Topical Requirements as a queue is going to age badly. The queue made sense when Cybersecurity was the only one with a date on it. It isn't anymore. The IIA released Third-Party with an effective date of September 15, 2026. Organizational Behavior follows on December 15, 2026. Organizational Resilience on April 30, 2027. Two more are moving through consultation behind them. So the plan that says "we'll do Cybersecurity this year and pick up Third-Party next year" is not a plan. It is a queue with four things in it and eighteen months of runway, and the requirements do not wait their turn. The part that gets missed: these overlap. Third-party access reviews are evidence for Cybersecurity. Escalation behavior shows up in both Organizational Behavior and Resilience. A function working the list sequentially tests the same control three times and reports it three ways. A function working it as one map tests it once. Whether your conformance is assessed one requirement at a time is not up to you. At your next external quality assessment it is assessed against what is in effect on that date, in whatever order you got there. Full stop. SO TEST IT, RIGHT NOW. We built a free screener that tells you where your function stands against each issued Topical Requirement. No call, no proposal, no salesperson. Answer the questions, see the gaps. [Run the screener] One more thing, since it is the same problem. We've added 19 new team members to run exactly this kind of coverage work. Yes, they're AI. More on that this week. Human-Led. Why the newsletter does not repeat the caption

Subscribers largely also follow the page. The social spotlight leads with what the service does; the newsletter leads with the argument for why the current way of working stopped holding, and only then names the service. Same week, same subject, different job.

The rules that apply to everything

No IIA logo

Off every public graphic, image and the newsletter. Black it out where it appears. Allowed, but Mike wants it kept to proposals only. It adds friction, not value, on social.

Attribute the IIA

Say "the IIA released..." explicitly in copy. Never anything that reads as if Cherry Hill wrote the Topical Requirements.

Shorter captions

Controversial hook first, then a checklist, not paragraphs. "For the good of the profession," not "buy our service."
