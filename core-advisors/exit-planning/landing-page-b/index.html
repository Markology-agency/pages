Why it happens

What a buyer is actually paying for

Every buyer is quietly asking one question: does this practice work when the owner isn't in it? Anything that only runs because you're standing there is something they have to replace, and they price that in every time.

That single question is what separates a practice that sells at a premium from one that sells at a discount. It's also why a practice can produce beautifully and still sell for less than its owner expected.

Buyers don't pay for how good a dentist you are. They pay for what keeps working after you hand over the keys.

What actually moves the number

A practice can pay you very well for years and still sell for less than you'd expect. Profit is what it pays you now; value is what someone else will pay for it later, and they're built differently.

These are the things a buyer looks for. Every one of them is something you can build on purpose, and every one of them makes the practice better to own in the meantime.

1

It runs without you

A strong associate and hygiene bench means production doesn't depend on you being chairside every day.

2

The systems are written down

Scheduling, hygiene recall, case presentation, billing, collections. Documented, and actually followed.

3

Patients belong to the practice

Relationships sit with the whole care team and the brand, not only with the owner-doctor.

4

There's a leadership bench

An office manager, a lead hygienist, a lead assistant. Decisions don't bottleneck at you.

5

The numbers are clean and current

Production, collections, hygiene %, case acceptance, chair utilization. Tracked, and acted on.

6

You have more than one way out

DSO, private buyer, partner buy-in, associate buy-in. Options exist when the value transfers with the practice.
