Findings

Each finding gives the cause, the evidence and how sure we are.

1. People opened the form and left Medium confidence

42 taps opened a form and none was sent. The first screen of the form stated the price ("A full quality of earnings report for $7,500 flat" or "A written year-end tax plan for $4,000"). It then asked for full name, email, phone number and two questions about the deal.

The September forms offered a free 30-minute deal screen or the webinar recording, and asked for a first name, an email and one question. On Facebook alone, the free offer turned 17 taps into forms from 3 people. The paid offer turned 18 Facebook taps into none. The numbers are small, which is why this is medium confidence.

What it means: a stranger on Facebook will give an email for something free. We have no sign yet that they will give a phone number against a $4,000 or $7,500 service.

2. One ad took half the budget High confidence

The inventory story ad ("Books said $1.2M. The count said $490K.") spent $83.00 of the $163.60 and drew 18 of the 42 taps. Meta decides which ad in a group gets shown, and it settled on this one on the first day. The other QoE ad ("A full QoE for $7,500, half our fee") was shown 44 times in total.

What it means: the inventory story is the ad people stop for. We learned nothing about the second QoE ad.

3. The credit and refund versions were never really compared High confidence

The franchise tax plan ran in two versions for three days. The refund version got $14.27 and 6 taps. The credit version got $3.13 and 2 taps. Neither got a form.

What it means: $17.40 can't tell us which wording franchise owners prefer. That question is still open.

4. Most of the money went out before the relaunch High confidence

The first run spent $104.66 in a little over three days at $40 a day. The relaunch, which carried the credit and refund wording from the 10/21 call, spent $58.94.

What it means: the offer as it stands today has had about $59 of testing on Meta.

5. Half the spend reached people 55 and over High confidence on the numbers

$81.27 of the $163.60 went to people 55 and over, and the 65 and over group was the largest single group at $43.64. We set the ads for ages 23 to 65 with an interest in entrepreneurship or a business started in the last six months. Meta's Advantage+ audience setting was on, and it allows Meta to show ads outside the ages and interests we choose.

Question for Darin: are the buyers you speak with mostly under 55? If so, the next test should run with that setting off so the age range is a hard limit.

6. Instagram has not produced a single form Medium confidence

Instagram took 67% of the October spend ($109.94) for 24 taps and no forms. In the September campaign it took $20.97 for 9 taps and no forms. Across both campaigns that is $130.91 and 33 taps without one form. Every form we can trace came from Facebook or from Audience Network.

What it means: the next test should run on Facebook only.

7. September's $17 leads did not turn into calls High confidence

The free-offer campaign in September and early October spent $307.22 and produced 18 forms, which looked like $17 a lead. Here is what was behind that number:

  • The 18 forms came from 15 people. One person sent the form 4 times.
  • 12 of the 18 came through Audience Network. That is Meta placing the ad inside other companies' apps and websites, where forms are often sent by accident.
  • 10 of the 15 people answered "Just exploring the idea". Two said they were in diligence, one had a deal under LOI, one was actively searching and one had already bought.
  • None of the 15 email addresses appears in the booked-calls sheet.

What it means: the October campaign ran on Facebook and Instagram only, so the source of most of September's forms was not in it. September should not be read as proof that Meta can deliver buyer leads at $17.

What we recommend

  1. Leave Meta paused for now. Running the paid offers again on the same forms would most likely repeat the October result.
  2. When Meta comes back, lead with the free deal screen. It is the only Meta offer that has brought in people with a live deal. The $7,500 QoE and the $4,000 tax plan can follow on the thank-you screen and in the follow-up emails.
  3. Get the welcome emails running first. A form lead who hears nothing afterward is unlikely to book. The emails are waiting on the choice of email platform (Mailchimp, Kit or Firm Pillar).
  4. Run the next test on Facebook only, with Advantage+ audience off. That removes Instagram and Audience Network and keeps the ads inside the age range we set.
  5. Keep the inventory story ad. It drew the most taps of anything we ran.
  6. Agree the test budget and end date before launch, and let it finish. We will judge it on booked buyer calls.
